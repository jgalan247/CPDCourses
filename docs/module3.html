Understanding Student AI Tools

Learn about the AI tools your students are actually using

The Reality Check

Your students are already using AI—whether we like it or not. ChatGPT, Quillbot, Grammarly, Photomath, and dozens of other tools are freely available on their phones. The question isn't "Should we allow AI?" but rather "How do we teach them to use it responsibly?"

💡 Shift in Mindset

Instead of fighting against AI use, we need to teach WITH it. Just as we taught students to evaluate Wikipedia, we now teach them to use AI as a learning tool—not a replacement for thinking.

Try It Yourself: Explore Student AI Tools

Time: 15 minutes

Task: Test Drive 2-3 Student Tools

Experience firsthand what your students are using. Try at least 2 tools from different categories:

Option 1: ChatGPT for homework

Ask it to write an essay on a topic you teach. See what it produces.

Option 2: Quillbot for paraphrasing

Copy a paragraph and see how it rephrases it. Would you catch this?

Option 3: Photomath for problem solving

Scan a math problem and review the step-by-step solution. Is it educational?

💡 Key Insight: Understanding these tools from a student's perspective is the first step in teaching responsible AI use. You can't guide what you don't understand!

Building Critical AI Literacy

Teach students to think critically about AI outputs

The 4 Pillars of AI Literacy

AI literacy isn't just about knowing how to use AI tools—it's about understanding what AI is, how it works, its limitations, and its impact on society. Here's what students need to learn:

1

Understanding AI

  • • What is AI? (Patterns in data, not magic)
  • • How does ChatGPT generate text?
  • • Why does AI sometimes "hallucinate" facts?
2

Evaluating AI Output

  • • Is this information accurate?
  • • What sources should I check?
  • • Is there bias in this response?
3

Using AI Responsibly

  • • When is AI use appropriate vs cheating?
  • • How do I cite AI-assisted work?
  • • What data am I sharing with AI tools?
4

AI & Society

  • • How will AI impact future careers?
  • • What are the ethical concerns with AI?
  • • How can AI perpetuate bias and inequality?

Practical Classroom Activities

🎓 Try These with Your Students:

The "Spot the Fake" Challenge

Give students 5 paragraphs—some AI-generated, some human-written. Can they tell the difference? Discuss clues.

The "Fact-Check Challenge"

Ask ChatGPT a question in your subject area. Have students verify every fact. How accurate is it?

The "Prompt Engineering" Game

Challenge students to get the best response from AI through improving their questions. Teach them effective prompting!
