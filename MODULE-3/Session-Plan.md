# Module 3: Session Plan
## Student-Facing AI Tools & Digital AI Literacy

**Duration:** 3 hours (180 minutes)
**Format:** Interactive workshop
**Audience:** Teachers (all subjects, all key stages)
**Prerequisite:** Module 1 or 2, or basic AI familiarity

---

## Session Overview

This session empowers teachers to guide students in using AI ethically, critically, and effectively. We shift from "teachers using AI" to "teaching students to use AI." The workshop balances opportunity with responsibility, skill-building with critical thinking.

### Session Philosophy: "Guide, Don't Gate"

- **Guide:** Teach responsible AI use rather than ban it
- **Equip:** Build critical AI literacy skills
- **Empower:** Prepare students for AI-integrated futures
- **Protect:** Maintain academic integrity and ethical use

## Detailed Session Timeline

### SEGMENT 1: Welcome & The AI Student Reality (0:00 - 0:20) - 20 minutes

#### 0:00-0:05 | Welcome & Context Setting (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Welcome warmly: "Today we focus on our students"
- Acknowledge the challenge: "Students are already using AI - often without guidance"
- Set the tone: "We're navigating new territory together"

**Key Message:** "The question isn't *if* students use AI, but *how* we teach them to use it well."

#### 0:05-0:10 | The Current Reality Check (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
Poll or discussion: "Have you seen students using AI in your classroom?"

**Show of hands:**
- Who has suspected AI-generated student work?
- Who has caught students using AI inappropriately?
- Who has seen students use AI well?
- Who feels confident addressing AI with students?

**Statistics to Share:**
- 89% of students have used ChatGPT for schoolwork (various surveys, 2023-24)
- Many don't know how to use it effectively or ethically
- Most lack critical evaluation skills
- Some use it to cheat; others use it to learn

**Key Insight:** "Students are digital natives with AI, but not AI-literate natives."

#### 0:10-0:20 | Activity 1: AI Literacy Skills Audit (10 mins)

**Objective:** Identify what students need to know about AI

**Instructions:**
1. In pairs or small groups
2. Brainstorm: "What should students know about AI?"
3. Categories:
   - **Knowledge:** What is AI? How does it work?
   - **Skills:** What can they do with AI?
   - **Ethics:** How should they use it responsibly?
   - **Critical Thinking:** How do they evaluate AI?

**Collect Responses** (Padlet/whiteboard):
- Common answers: fact-checking, prompting, bias awareness, citation, privacy

**Facilitator Summary:**
"These are AI literacy skills. Today, we'll learn to teach them."

**Transition:** "Before we teach students, let's explore the tools they're using..."

### SEGMENT 2: Student AI Tools Exploration (0:20 - 0:50) - 30 minutes

#### 0:20-0:25 | The Student AI Landscape (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Explain: "Students have access to many AI tools, not just ChatGPT"
- Show categories:
  - **Research & Learning:** Perplexity, Claude, ChatGPT
  - **Writing Support:** Grammarly, QuillBot, Wordtune
  - **Creative:** DALL-E, Midjourney, Canva AI
  - **Study:** Quizlet AI, Photomath, Study assistants
  - **Accessibility:** Text-to-speech, translation, summaries

**Key Point:** "Some tools are helpful learning aids. Others can undermine learning if misused."

#### 0:25-0:40 | Student Tools Demonstration (15 mins)

**Facilitator Actions - Demonstrate 5 Student Tools:**

**Demo 1: ChatGPT for Research & Learning (3 mins)**
- Show: Student research question: "Explain photosynthesis for my Year 8 project"
- Highlight: Good use vs. bad use
  - ✅ Good: "Explain this concept" → "Now quiz me"
  - ❌ Bad: "Write my essay" → Copy-paste
- Teach: "AI as tutor, not author"

**Demo 2: Perplexity AI for Research (3 mins)**
- Show: Research query with citations
- Highlight: Built-in source citations
- Advantage: Encourages verification
- Student use case: Starting research, finding sources

**Demo 3: Quill Bot (Paraphrasing Tool) (3 mins)**
- Show: How students might use it
- Discuss: When is paraphrasing learning? When is it plagiarism?
- The gray area: Understanding vs. hiding sources

**Demo 4: Grammarly or Language Tools (3 mins)**
- Show: Writing assistance features
- Discuss: Is grammar checking "cheating"?
- Where's the line: Proofreading vs. rewriting?

**Demo 5: Creative AI (DALL-E, Canva AI) (3 mins)**
- Show: Image generation for projects
- Discuss: Citation of AI-generated images
- Appropriate use: Inspiration, mockups, creativity

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"Each tool can be used well or poorly. Our job: teach discernment."

#### 0:40-0:50 | Activity 2: Student Tools Exploration (10 mins)

**Objective:** Hands-on exploration of student AI tools

**Instructions:**
1. Choose 2-3 tools to explore:
   - Research tool (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude)
   - Writing assistant (Grammarly, Quill Bot)
   - Creative tool (Canva AI, image generator)
   - Study tool (Quizlet AI, Photomath)

2. Try a student task:
   - Research a topic from your subject
   - Get writing feedback
   - Create a visual
   - Generate study materials

3. Consider:
   - How could a student use this well?
   - How might they misuse it?
   - What guidance would you give?

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Circulate and discuss
- Note interesting examples
- Address concerns

**Share Time (last 2 mins):**
- Quick poll: "Which tool surprised you most?"
- 1-2 people share: "One insight about student AI use"

**Transition:** "Now that we know the tools, let's build AI literacy..."

### SEGMENT 3: Teaching AI Literacy & Critical Thinking (1:00 - 1:30) - 30 minutes

#### 1:00-1:05 | AI Literacy Framework (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
Present the **4 Pillars of Student AI Literacy:**

**1. Understanding AI**
- What is AI? How does it work (simply)?
- What can AI do? What can't it do?
- Where does AI information come from?

**2. Critical Evaluation**
- Is this information accurate?
- What biases might exist?
- What's missing from this AI response?
- How do I verify?

**3. Ethical Use**
- When is AI use appropriate?
- How do I cite AI assistance?
- What's academic integrity with AI?
- Privacy and data concerns

**4. Effective Use**
- How do I prompt effectively?
- How do I use AI to learn (not avoid learning)?
- How do I combine AI with my own thinking?

**Display Visual:** Framework diagram with examples

#### 1:05-1:20 | Teaching AI Literacy - Strategies (15 mins)

**Facilitator Actions - Demonstrate Lesson Strategies:**

**Strategy 1: "AI Says..." Challenge (3 mins)**
- Give students AI responses with errors
- Task: Find the mistakes
- Teaches: Verification, critical evaluation
- Age: KS2+

**Example:**
"Ask ChatGPT about the Battle of Hastings. Check its answer against your textbook. What did it get right? Wrong?"

**Strategy 2: Prompt Engineering Practice (3 mins)**
- Teach students better prompting
- Bad prompt → Good prompt comparison
- Teaches: How AI works, effective use
- Age: KS3+

**Example:**
- Bad: "Essay on climate change"
- Good: "Explain 3 main causes of climate change for a Year 9 Geography project. Include examples."

**Strategy 3: "AI as Research Partner" Protocol (3 mins)**
- Step 1: Use AI to understand topic
- Step 2: Identify questions AI raised
- Step 3: Research credible sources
- Step 4: Synthesize your own understanding
- Teaches: AI as starting point, not endpoint
- Age: KS3+

**Strategy 4: Bias Detection Activity (3 mins)**
- Ask AI same question multiple ways
- Compare responses
- Identify differences and why
- Teaches: AI limitations, bias awareness
- Age: KS4+

**Example:**
"Ask AI about a controversial topic from multiple perspectives. How do responses differ?"

**Strategy 5: "I Used AI" Reflection (3 mins)**
- When students use AI, they reflect:
  - How did I use AI?
  - What did I learn?
  - How did AI help/hinder?
  - What did I add that AI couldn't?
- Teaches: Metacognition, accountability
- Age: All

#### 1:20-1:30 | Activity 3: Design an AI Literacy Lesson (10 mins)

**Objective:** Create a 15-minute AI literacy lesson for your students

**Instructions:**
Choose your focus:
- **Option A:** "Understanding AI" - What is it? How does it work?
- **Option B:** "Critical Evaluation" - Fact-checking AI responses
- **Option C:** "Ethical Use" - When to use AI, how to cite
- **Option D:** "Effective Use" - Prompt engineering basics

**Design your lesson:**
1. Learning objective
2. Quick activity (5-10 mins)
3. Discussion prompts
4. Takeaway for students

**Use template provided**

**Share Time (last 2 mins):**
- Table groups share ideas
- One volunteer shares with whole group

**Transition:** "AI literacy is essential. But we also need to address misuse..."

### SEGMENT 4: Detecting & Addressing AI-Generated Work (1:30 - 2:00) - 30 minutes

#### 1:30-1:35 | The Detection Reality (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
**Honest Conversation:**

"Can we detect AI-generated work? Sometimes. Should we become AI police? No."

**Key Points:**
- AI detection tools are unreliable (false positives/negatives)
- Students can usually bypass detection tools
- Over-reliance on detection damages trust
- Focus: Teaching, not policing

**But... we still need strategies:**
- Recognize potential AI use
- Have conversations
- Design assignments AI can't easily do
- Respond constructively

#### 1:35-1:50 | Detecting AI Work - Practical Strategies (15 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**

**Strategy 1: Linguistic Markers (5 mins)**
Show examples of AI-generated text:

**Common AI Markers:**
- Overly formal or generic language
- Perfect grammar, lacks personal voice
- Balanced to the point of blandness
- Lists and structure (AI loves organizing)
- Certain phrases: "delve into," "explore," "it's important to note"
- Lack of specific, personal examples
- Sudden style shift from student's usual work

**Practice:** Show 3 paragraphs - which is AI-generated?
(Participants guess, discuss indicators)

**Strategy 2: The Conversation Approach (3 mins)**
**Most reliable detection: Talk to the student**

Questions to ask:
- "Tell me about your research process?"
- "What was challenging about this?"
- "Explain this point in your own words?"
- "What sources did you use?"

If they can't explain or elaborate → likely misused AI

**Strategy 3: Assignment Design (4 mins)**
**Design AI-resistant assignments:**

❌ **Easy for AI:**
- Generic essay prompts
- Factual summaries
- Standard format reports

✅ **Hard for AI:**
- Personal reflection and connection
- In-class observations and experiments
- Process portfolios (show thinking development)
- Specific, local examples required
- Oral presentations with Q&A
- Time-pressured tasks
- Iterative work with teacher feedback

**Strategy 4: AI Detection Tools (3 mins)**
**Show tools but explain limitations:**
- GPTZero, Turnitin AI detector, etc.
- Demo: They're not reliable
- False positives harm innocent students
- False negatives miss AI use
- Use cautiously, never as sole evidence

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"Prevention through good teaching > Detection through surveillance"

#### 1:50-2:00 | Activity 4: Responding to AI Misuse (10 mins)

**Objective:** Practice conversations about AI misuse

**Instructions:**

**Scenario 1:** You suspect a student used AI to write their essay. How do you approach?

**Scenario 2:** A student admits they used AI but didn't know it was wrong. Now what?

**Scenario 3:** A parent complains their child got a zero for AI use. They argue "everyone uses AI."

**In pairs:**
1. Choose one scenario
2. Role-play the conversation
3. Focus on: Education, not punishment

**Guiding Principles:**
- Assume positive intent first
- Focus on learning opportunity
- Clarify expectations
- Teach, don't shame

**Share (last 2 mins):**
- What approaches worked?
- What's challenging about these conversations?

**Transition:** "Let's move from challenges to opportunities - designing great AI-enhanced lessons..."

### SEGMENT 5: Design AI-Enhanced Lessons (2:10 - 2:40) - 30 minutes

#### 2:10-2:15 | AI-Enhanced vs. AI-Replaced Learning (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**
**Key Distinction:**

❌ **AI-Replaced:** AI does the thinking/work
✅ **AI-Enhanced:** AI supports deeper learning

**Examples:**

**AI-Replaced (Avoid):**
- "Use ChatGPT to write your essay"
- AI generates, student submits

**AI-Enhanced (Encourage):**
- "Use ChatGPT to brainstorm ideas. Then write your essay incorporating 3 ideas plus your own."
- AI assists, student thinks

**Framework: SAMR Model for AI**
- **Substitution:** AI does what student would do (avoid)
- **Augmentation:** AI improves student's process (okay)
- **Modification:** AI enables new approaches (good)
- **Redefinition:** AI enables previously impossible tasks (excellent)

#### 2:15-2:35 | Activity 5: Design Your AI-Enhanced Lesson (20 mins)

**Objective:** Create a lesson that uses student-facing AI effectively

**Instructions:**

**Step 1: Choose Your Lesson (2 mins)**
- Pick an upcoming lesson/unit
- Consider: Where could AI add value?

**Step 2: Design AI Integration (10 mins)**

Use the template:
1. **Learning Objective:** What should students learn?
2. **AI Tool/Feature:** Which AI will they use?
3. **Student Task:** What will students DO with AI?
4. **Learning Activity:** How does AI support (not replace) learning?
5. **Assessment:** How will you know they learned?
6. **AI Guidelines:** Clear rules for AI use
7. **Reflection:** How will students reflect on AI use?

**Examples to inspire:**

**Science (KS3): States of Matter**
- AI Use: ChatGPT to generate quiz questions on states of matter
- Task: Students evaluate AI questions for accuracy, improve them
- Learning: Understanding concepts deeply enough to critique

**English (KS4): Character Analysis**
- AI Use: Claude to discuss character motivations
- Task: Debate with AI, find evidence AI missed
- Learning: Critical reading, argumentation

**Maths (KS3): Problem-Solving**
- AI Use: ChatGPT to explain solution steps
- Task: Solve problem, compare method to AI's, identify differences
- Learning: Mathematical reasoning, multiple strategies

**History (KS4): Source Analysis**
- AI Use: ChatGPT for historical context
- Task: Use AI for background, analyze sources independently
- Learning: Research skills, source evaluation

**Step 3: Consider Potential Issues (3 mins)**
- What could go wrong?
- How will you prevent AI misuse?
- What if AI gives wrong information?

**Step 4: Refine (5 mins)**
- Review your lesson design
- Make it specific and actionable
- Add one "what if" contingency

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Circulate and provide feedback
- Help refine ideas
- Note excellent examples

**Share Time (last 5 mins):**
- Gallery walk (post lesson plans)
- OR table shares
- OR 2-3 volunteers present to whole group

**Facilitator Highlights:**
- Celebrate creativity
- Note common themes
- Emphasize: AI enhances, doesn't replace

**Transition:** "You've designed lessons. Now let's create policies..."

### SEGMENT 6: Classroom AI Policy & Action Planning (2:40 - 3:00) - 20 minutes

#### 2:40-2:50 | Creating Student AI Use Policies (10 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**

**Why Policies Matter:**
- Clarity prevents confusion
- Students need explicit guidance
- Parents need transparency
- You need consistency

**Template Policy Framework:**

**1. Purpose Statement**
"AI tools can support learning when used ethically and critically."

**2. When AI Use is Encouraged**
- Brainstorming and ideation
- Research starting points
- Checking understanding
- Study and revision

**3. When AI Use is Prohibited**
- Summative assessments (unless specified)
- Original creative work
- Personal reflections
- In-class timed tasks

**4. Citation Requirements**
"If you use AI, cite it like any source: 'Generated with ChatGPT, [date]'"

**5. Consequences**
- First time: Educational conversation
- Repeated: [School policy]

**Quick Activity (5 mins):**

**Create your classroom AI policy:**
Use template, adapt for your context

**Share:**
- Pair-share policies
- Discuss: What works? What's challenging?

**Facilitator Summary:**
"Policies aren't punishment - they're teaching tools."

#### 2:50-2:55 | Communicating with Students & Parents (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**

**For Students:**
- Explicit lesson on AI policy (first week)
- Model responsible AI use
- Regular reminders and discussions
- Celebrate good AI use

**For Parents:**
- Clear communication (newsletter, email)
- Explain the "why"
- Invite questions
- Regular updates

**Sample Parent Communication:**

"Dear Parents,

AI tools like ChatGPT are becoming common. Rather than ban them, we're teaching students to use them ethically and critically. Your child will learn to:
- Use AI as a learning assistant, not a replacement for thinking
- Verify AI information
- Cite AI use appropriately
- Understand when AI use is/isn't appropriate

If you have questions, please ask. We're navigating this together."

#### 2:55-3:00 | Action Planning & Close (5 mins)

**Facilitator Actions:**

**Immediate Actions:**
- This week: Try one AI literacy activity
- This month: Implement one AI-enhanced lesson
- This term: Establish classroom AI policy

**Action Plan Template** (distributed):
1. One AI literacy lesson I'll teach:
2. One AI-enhanced lesson I'll design:
3. My classroom AI policy includes:
4. I'll communicate this to students/parents by:

**Post-Workshop Evaluation:**
- Complete 2-minute survey
- Share your experiences in community group

**Final Message:**

"Students are growing up in an AI world. Our job isn't to shield them from AI, but to prepare them to use it wisely.

You now have strategies to:
- Teach AI literacy
- Guide responsible use
- Detect and address misuse
- Design AI-enhanced lessons

Start small. Be brave. Iterate. We're figuring this out together."

**Thank participants, open for final questions**

## Facilitator Quick Reference

### Time Management
- AI tools demos can run long - set timer
- Activity 5 (lesson design) is crucial - don't rush
- Can shorten detection section if needed
- Policy creation can be homework if time short

### Common Issues

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| "We should just ban AI" | Acknowledge concern; discuss why guidance > gatekeeping |
| Tools won't load | Have screenshots/videos backup |
| Moral panic about AI | Validate feelings; focus on practical strategies |
| "This is too permissive" | Emphasize academic integrity; show boundaries |
| "This is too restrictive" | Explain learning goals; show where AI helps |
| Split room on approach | Facilitate respectful discussion; no single answer |

### Key Messages to Reinforce
- AI literacy is essential for students' futures
- Teaching responsible use > banning
- Academic integrity remains crucial
- Start small, iterate, learn together
- No perfect answers - we're pioneering
