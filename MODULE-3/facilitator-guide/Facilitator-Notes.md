# Facilitator Guide: Module 3
## Student-Facing AI Tools & Digital AI Literacy

**Welcome, Facilitator!** This module addresses one of education's most pressing challenges: preparing students to use AI responsibly in an AI-saturated world.

---

## Before You Begin

### Your Role

You're facilitating difficult conversations with no perfect answers. Teachers will have strong, varied opinions:
- Some want to ban AI entirely
- Some embrace it fully
- Most are somewhere in the middle, uncertain

Your job: **Provide frameworks, strategies, and confidence - not absolute answers.**

### Key Principles

1. **No judgment** - Teachers are navigating new territory
2. **Balanced approach** - Neither ban nor free-for-all
3. **Practical focus** - Less philosophy, more "what do I do Monday?"
4. **Acknowledge uncertainty** - We're all learning
5. **Student-centered** - Always return to "What's best for learning?"

## Facilitation Tips by Section

### Opening (0:00-0:20): The Student Reality

**Tone:** Empathetic and realistic

**Key Actions:**
- Acknowledge fears and concerns immediately
- Share statistics about student AI use
- Frame as "opportunity + challenge" not just threat

**Common Opening Reactions:**
- **Panic:** "Students are cheating everywhere!"
  → Validate, then reframe: "Let's focus on what we can do"

- **Dismissal:** "This is overblown"
  → Share data: "89% of students report using ChatGPT"

- **Confusion:** "I don't know where to start"
  → "That's why we're here. We'll build practical strategies together."

**Critical First 10 Minutes:**
Establish that this workshop is:
- ✅ Practical, not theoretical
- ✅ Balanced, not extreme
- ✅ Supportive, not judgmental

---

### Student Tools Exploration (0:20-0:50)

**Tone:** Curious and exploratory

**Demonstration Tips:**
1. **Show real student use cases** - not abstract examples
2. **Demonstrate both good and bad use** - side by side
3. **Name the dilemma** - "This is a gray area. Here's how to think about it."

**Tools to Prioritize:**
- ChatGPT (they all know it)
- Perplexity (research - often better for students)
- Grammarly (ubiquitous, raises "where's the line?" questions)
- One creative tool (Canva AI or similar)

**Common Questions:**

**Q: "Should we block these tools on the school network?"**
A: "Students have phones. Blocking doesn't work long-term. Teaching discernment does."

**Q: "Isn't using Grammarly cheating?"**
A: "Great question for discussion. Where do you draw the line? Is spell-check cheating? What about a thesaurus? These are judgment calls we make explicit."

**Q: "What if parents buy students ChatGPT Plus?"**
A: "They will. Our job: teach students to use it as a learning tool, not a shortcut."

**Activity Tips:**
- Pair less confident teachers with tech-savvy ones
- Circulate actively - many will need reassurance
- Note resistance - address in ethics discussion
- Celebrate "aha moments"

---

### AI Literacy Teaching (1:00-1:30)

**Tone:** Empowering and practical

**This is the heart of the workshop.**

**Key Message:** "We can't stop students using AI. We CAN teach them to use it well."

**The 4 Pillars Framework:**
Present clearly with visuals. Teachers need simple, memorable frameworks.

**Demonstration Tips:**
- Use teacher's own subject examples when possible
- Ask: "How would you adapt this for your year group?"
- Emphasize: These are lessons, not one-off talks

**Activity 3 Support:**
Teachers designing AI literacy lessons need:
- Clear templates
- Multiple examples
- Permission to start small: "A 10-minute activity is enough"

**Common Challenges:**

**Challenge: "I don't have time to teach AI literacy"**
Response: "Integration, not addition. 10 minutes once a term builds awareness."

**Challenge: "What if I don't understand AI well enough?"**
Response: "Learn with your students. Model curiosity. You don't need to be an expert."

**Challenge: "My students already know more than me"**
Response: "They know how to USE it. You teach them to THINK about it. That's different."

---

### Detecting AI Work (1:30-2:00)

**Tone:** Realistic and measured

**⚠️ SENSITIVE SECTION ⚠️**

Many teachers want foolproof detection methods. You must:
1. Acknowledge the desire
2. Explain the reality (unreliable detection)
3. Offer better approaches (assignment design, conversations)

**Critical Points:**
- **AI detectors are unreliable** - Demo this! Show false positives.
- **Detection arms race is unwinnable** - Students adapt faster
- **Focus on prevention** - Better than detection

**Linguistic Markers Demo:**
- Show 3 paragraphs (2 human, 1 AI)
- Have participants guess
- Reveal answer
- Discuss markers they noticed
- **Important:** Emphasize markers aren't foolproof

**The Conversation Approach:**
Frame as **pedagogically sound**, not interrogation:
- "Tell me about your process" is good teaching anyway
- Understanding > accusation
- Opportunity to teach

**Assignment Design:**
This is the most empowering part:
- Show how AI-resistant assignments are often BETTER assignments
- Personal connection, process, iteration = deeper learning anyway
- Reframe: Not "AI-proofing" but "learning-deepening"

**AI Detection Tools:**
- Demo one (GPTZero or Turnitin)
- Show limitations clearly
- **Warn against over-reliance**
- Never use as sole evidence

**Scenario Role-Plays:**
- These are valuable but can be uncomfortable
- Set ground rules: Respectful, learning-focused
- Debrief: "What was hard? What worked?"
- Emphasize: Educate, don't punish

---

### Designing AI Lessons (2:10-2:40)

**Tone:** Creative and inspiring

**This is the fun part!**

**Key Distinction:**
AI-Enhanced vs. AI-Replaced must be crystal clear.

Use concrete examples from multiple subjects.

**Activity 5 Support:**
- **This activity is CRUCIAL** - don't rush
- Circulate extensively
- Ask probing questions:
  - "How does AI support learning here, not replace it?"
  - "What will students understand better because of AI?"
  - "How will you assess learning, not AI output?"

**Encourage Creativity:**
- Celebrate innovative ideas
- Share examples across subjects
- Build excitement

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Lesson is actually AI-replaced (AI does the work)
  → Push back gently: "What's the learning goal here?"

- Too ambitious for first try
  → "Start smaller. One activity. Build from there."

- Unclear assessment
  → "How will you know they learned?"

**Gallery Walk:**
- Take photos (with permission) - these become resources
- Note standout examples to share later
- Create culture of sharing

---

### Policy & Closing (2:40-3:00)

**Tone:** Actionable and confident

**Policy Creation:**
- Templates are starting points, not mandates
- Every context is different
- Align with school policy
- Keep it simple

**Communication:**
- Model sample parent communication
- Emphasize transparency
- Encourage questions

**Action Planning:**
- Make it concrete: specific lessons, dates, people
- Start small: "One thing this week"
- Follow-up: Join community, share experiences

**Final Message:**
End on empowerment, not fear:
- "You have strategies"
- "You're not alone"
- "Start small, iterate"
- "We're figuring this out together"

## Handling Difficult Moments

### Scenario: Strong Anti-AI Sentiment

**Teacher says:** "AI is ruining education. We should ban it entirely."

**Response:**
"I hear that concern. Many feel that way. Can I ask: Do your students have phones? Internet at home?
[They'll say yes]
Then they have AI access regardless of school policy. Given that reality, what's our best approach - ignore it, or teach responsible use?"

**Follow-up:** "Banning doesn't stop use. It stops conversation. Wouldn't you rather students learn from you?"

### Scenario: Overly Permissive View

**Teacher says:** "AI is just a tool like a calculator. Let them use it for everything."

**Response:**
"Tools have appropriate and inappropriate uses. A calculator is great for arithmetic, but we still teach mathematical reasoning. Where's the learning goal? That guides when tools help vs. hinder."

**Follow-up:** "If AI writes the essay, what does the student learn about writing?"

### Scenario: Overwhelmed Teacher

**Teacher says:** "This is too much. I can't keep up. I'm just going to stick with traditional methods."

**Response:**
"Completely understandable. You don't need to do everything today. What if you just started with one thing: Have one 10-minute conversation with students about AI? That's it. Just awareness. Would that feel manageable?"

**Follow-up:** "Small steps. You're not behind. We're all learning."

### Scenario: The Denier

**Teacher says:** "My students don't use AI. This isn't an issue in my class."

**Response:**
"That's possible. May I share some data? [Show statistics] In most surveys, 60-90% of students report using AI tools. How might you open a conversation to find out? No judgment - just curiosity?"

**Follow-up:** "Sometimes students don't share because they fear punishment. Creating safe space to discuss can be revealing."

### Scenario: Tech Anxiety

**Teacher says:** "I don't understand this technology well enough to teach it."

**Response:**
"You don't need to be an expert. You're not teaching computer science. You're teaching: 'Think critically. Verify information. Use tools responsibly.' You do that already with internet research, right? This is similar."

**Follow-up:** "In fact, learning alongside students models lifelong learning. They respect that."

## Time Management

### If Running Behind:
- **Cut:** Some tool demos (focus on ChatGPT, Perplexity)
- **Shorten:** Detection section (key points only)
- **Combine:** Policy creation becomes homework
- **Keep:** AI literacy teaching and lesson design (core value)

### If Running Ahead:
- **Extend:** Lesson design activity (more refinement)
- **Add:** More scenario role-plays
- **Deepen:** Policy discussion (compare approaches)
- **Q&A:** Open floor for questions

## Differentiation for Participants

### By Comfort Level:
- **Anxious:** Pair with confident colleague, provide templates
- **Confident:** Extension challenges, help support others
- **Skeptical:** Listen, validate, provide evidence-based responses

### By Key Stage:
- **Primary (KS1-2):** Focus on simple AI literacy, very guided use
- **Secondary (KS3-4):** Balance guidance with independence
- **Sixth Form (KS5):** Emphasis on academic integrity, uni prep

### By Subject:
Have ready examples from:
- English (research, writing, analysis)
- Science (explaining concepts, generating questions)
- Maths (problem-solving explanations, error checking)
- Humanities (research, multiple perspectives)
- Languages (translation, practice, feedback)
- Arts (inspiration, mockups, critique)

## Self-Care for Facilitators

### This Module is Emotionally Charged

Teachers feel:
- Anxious (tech changing fast)
- Guilty (if students cheated and they didn't catch it)
- Defensive (if they use AI themselves but police students)
- Overwhelmed (another thing to learn)
- Excited (new opportunities)

**Your job:** Hold space for all these feelings

**You might feel:**
- Uncertain (you don't have all answers either)
- Defensive (if challenged)
- Overwhelmed (by questions you can't answer)

**Remember:**
- You're a facilitator, not an oracle
- "I don't know, let's explore that" is a valid answer
- Share the learning journey
- Model flexibility
