# Activity 3: Feedback Generation Workshop

**Duration:** 15 minutes
**Format:** Hands-on practice with real or sample student work
**Objective:** Generate personalized, quality feedback efficiently using AI

---

## Activity Overview

Participants learn to use AI to draft feedback for student work, then personalize it with their professional judgment and knowledge of individual students. This tackles one of teachers' biggest time drains while maintaining feedback quality.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this activity, participants will be able to:
- Use AI tools to generate draft feedback for student work
- Edit and personalize AI feedback appropriately
- Adapt feedback tone for different students
- Create feedback templates for common assessment types
- Understand the ethical boundaries of AI-generated feedback

---

## Pre-Activity Setup

### Facilitator Preparation
1. Prepare 5-10 anonymized student work samples across subjects:
   - Science lab report (middle ability)
   - English essay excerpt (needs improvement)
   - Math problem-solving (strong work)
   - History source analysis (mixed quality)
   - Art project description (creative but unclear)

2. Create example AI feedback (good and poor quality) for discussion

3. Have prompt templates ready on screen

4. Set up document sharing for participants to post examples

### Privacy & Ethics Reminder
**Before starting, emphasize:**
- "Remove all student names and identifying information"
- "Do not input sensitive data into AI tools"
- "This is about drafting, not publishing without review"

## Activity Instructions

### Part 1: Watch & Learn (3 mins)

**Facilitator Demonstration:**

**Demo:** Generate feedback for a sample essay excerpt

**Step 1: Set Up the Prompt**
```
You are providing feedback on a Year 9 English essay about themes in "Of Mice and Men."

Student work excerpt:
[Paste 150-word sample]

Provide encouraging, constructive feedback that:
- Highlights 2 specific strengths
- Identifies 2 areas for improvement with examples
- Suggests 1 concrete next step
- Uses student-friendly, supportive language
- Focuses on: thesis clarity, evidence use, and analysis depth

Word count: 100-150 words
Tone: Encouraging but honest
```

**Step 2: Review AI Output**
Show the generated feedback

**Step 3: Personalize**
Demonstrate adding:
- Student name
- Specific reference: "Your point about Candy in paragraph 2..."
- Personal knowledge: "Building on your improvement from last essay..."
- Encouragement matched to student: "This shows real progress..."

**Before AI-Only:**
"Your essay shows good understanding of the text. You made some strong points about loneliness. Your evidence use could be stronger. Try to analyze quotes more deeply. Your conclusion needs development. Keep working on structure. Overall, this is solid work."

**After Personalization:**
"Jamie, your analysis of loneliness through Candy's character (paragraph 2) really stood out - you're making strong textual connections! Your evidence selection is thoughtful. To develop further, try the 'PEE' structure we practiced: when you quote 'I got no relatives,' push yourself to explain *why* Steinbeck chose those specific words. Also, your conclusion currently summarizes, but I'd love to see you link back to your opening question about whether the American Dream is achievable. This essay shows real progress from your last one - your paragraphs are much more focused now!"

**Facilitator Points:**
- "Notice the difference? The AI draft is generic. After personalization, it's specific and encouraging."
- "This took 2 minutes total. Writing from scratch? 5-7 minutes. Scale that across 30 students..."

---

### Part 2: Hands-On Practice (10 mins)

**Instructions to Participants:**

**Choose Your Path:**

**Option A: Use Provided Samples**
- Select 2-3 sample student works from the resource pack
- Generate feedback for each
- Personalize the feedback

**Option B: Use Your Own Student Work**
- Bring anonymized work from your class
- Generate feedback
- Adapt to your specific students

**Process to Follow:**

**Step 1: Select Student Work (1 min)**
Choose work that:
- Represents common quality level in your class
- You actually need to give feedback on soon
- Is appropriate to analyze (not too personal/sensitive)

**Step 2: Create Your Prompt (2 mins)**
Use this template:
```
You are providing feedback on [assignment type] for Year [X] [subject].

Student work:
[Paste or describe work]

Provide feedback that:
- Highlights [number] strengths with specific examples
- Identifies [number] areas for improvement
- Suggests [number] next steps
- Tone: [encouraging/constructive/firm but supportive]
- Focus on: [specific criteria, e.g., "argument strength, evidence use, writing clarity"]

Length: [word count]
```

**Step 3: Generate Feedback (1 min)**
- Paste prompt into AI tool
- Review output
- Note what's good and what needs changing

**Step 4: Personalize (4 mins)**
Edit the feedback to add:
- ✏️ Student name or identifier
- ✏️ Specific reference to their work ("Your diagram on page 2...")
- ✏️ Connection to prior learning ("Remember when we looked at...")
- ✏️ Personal encouragement matched to student needs
- ✏️ School/class-specific language

**Step 5: Quality Check (2 mins)**
Ask yourself:
- Is this feedback I'd be proud to give?
- Is it specific enough to be actionable?
- Does it sound like me, not a robot?
- Would the student know what to do next?
- Is the tone appropriate for this student?

---

### Part 3: Share & Refine (2 mins)

**Pair-Share:**
1. Find a partner (ideally same or similar subject)
2. Share one piece of feedback you created
3. Partner gives feedback on the feedback:
   - What's strong?
   - What could be more specific?
   - Does it sound human and personal?

**Whole Group:**
- 2 volunteers share an example
- Facilitator highlights quality elements
- Discuss: "What made the personalization effective?"

## Subject-Specific Prompt Templates

### Science Feedback

**Lab Report/Practical Write-Up:**
```
Provide feedback on a Year [X] science practical report on [topic].

Focus areas:
- Hypothesis clarity
- Method detail and sequencing
- Results recording and presentation
- Analysis and conclusion quality
- Scientific vocabulary use

Student work:
[Paste excerpt or summary]

Feedback should: Highlight 2 strengths, identify 2 improvements, suggest 1 next step for scientific writing.
Tone: Encouraging and scientific.
Length: 120 words.
```

### Mathematics Feedback

**Problem-Solving Work:**
```
Provide feedback on Year [X] student's math problem-solving work on [topic].

Student's work:
[Describe approach, calculations, answer]

Focus on:
- Correct method selection
- Working-out clarity
- Calculation accuracy
- Reasoning and explanation
- Answer presentation

Feedback should: Praise correct approaches, identify where errors occurred, suggest strategies to avoid similar mistakes.
Be specific about mathematical process.
Tone: Supportive and precise.
Length: 100 words.
```

### English/Humanities Feedback

**Essay/Extended Writing:**
```
Provide feedback on a Year [X] [subject] essay on [topic].

Student work excerpt:
[Paste 200-300 words]

Assessment criteria:
- Argument/thesis strength
- Evidence and examples
- Analysis depth
- Structure and coherence
- Technical accuracy (spelling, grammar, punctuation)

Feedback should: 2-3 specific strengths, 2 areas to develop with examples from their work, 1 clear next step.
Tone: Encouraging, academic.
Length: 150 words.
```

### Creative Subjects (Art, DT, Drama, Music)

**Project/Performance Work:**
```
Provide feedback on Year [X] [subject] project: [description].

What student created:
[Describe work]

Assessment criteria:
- Creativity and originality
- Technical skill
- Development process
- Final outcome quality
- Reflection on work

Feedback should: Celebrate creative choices, note technical strengths, suggest refinements, encourage experimentation.
Tone: Enthusiastic and constructive.
Length: 120 words.
```

## Differentiated Feedback Approaches

### Adapting Feedback for Different Students

**For Students with Anxiety:**
```
Adjust this feedback for a student with anxiety who is very self-critical.
Tone: Highly encouraging, gentle on areas for improvement, emphasize progress and effort.
Frame improvements as exciting opportunities, not failures.
```

**For EAL Students:**
```
Adapt this feedback for an EAL student with intermediate English.
Use simpler vocabulary, shorter sentences, avoid idioms.
Include specific examples from their work.
Acknowledge language development alongside subject content.
```

**For High-Achieving Students:**
```
Adapt this feedback for a high-achieving student who needs challenge.
Acknowledge strong work, but push thinking further.
Suggest extension questions, deeper analysis, cross-curricular connections.
Tone: Respectful of ability, intellectually challenging.
```

**For Students with SEND:**
```
Adapt this feedback for a student with [specific need, e.g., dyslexia, ADHD, autism].
Focus on content over presentation (unless presentation is the skill being taught).
Use clear, structured language.
Break improvements into small, specific steps.
Celebrate effort and progress.
```

## Bulk Feedback Strategies

### For When You Have 30+ Pieces to Mark

**Approach 1: Category-Based Feedback**

1. Sort work into 3-4 categories:
   - Exceeds expectations
   - Meets expectations
   - Needs improvement
   - Needs significant support

2. Create template feedback for each category using AI

3. Personalize each individual piece with:
   - Specific example from their work
   - Student name
   - 1-2 sentences unique to them

**Approach 2: Criteria-Focused Feedback**

1. Assess each piece against 3-4 criteria

2. Use AI to generate feedback snippets for each criterion:
   - Strong thesis: [Template A]
   - Weak thesis: [Template B]
   - Good evidence: [Template C]
   - Weak evidence: [Template D]

3. Mix and match snippets + personalize

**Approach 3: Two-Tier Feedback**

1. AI generates whole-class feedback:
   - Common strengths across all work
   - Most frequent errors
   - General next steps

2. Individual feedback focuses only on what's unique to that student

## Quality Indicators: Good vs. Poor Feedback

### Poor AI Feedback (Unedited)

❌ "Good effort on this assignment. You demonstrated understanding of the topic. Some areas could use improvement. Continue to work on your analysis skills. Overall, satisfactory work."

**Problems:**
- Vague and generic
- No specific examples
- Could apply to any student
- No actionable next steps
- Sounds robotic

### Good AI-Assisted Feedback (Personalized)

✅ "Aisha, your explanation of osmosis using the potato experiment was clear and accurate - particularly your point about water moving from high to low concentration. To strengthen further, try explaining *why* this happens at a molecular level (think about our lesson on diffusion). Your diagram was helpful, but adding labels for 'before' and 'after' would make it even clearer. Next practical, challenge yourself to predict results before we start - I think you're ready for that!"

**Strengths:**
- Specific to student and work
- Names exact strengths
- Clear, actionable improvement
- Encouraging and personalized tone
- Sounds like a teacher who knows the student

## Ethical Considerations Discussion

### Questions to Pose:

1. **Transparency:** "Should students know AI helped draft feedback?"
   - Discuss: Opinions vary; transparency builds trust

2. **Authenticity:** "How do we keep feedback feeling human?"
   - Answer: Personalization is key

3. **Equity:** "Does AI feedback advantage some students over others?"
   - Consider: May reduce bias if used carefully; may introduce new biases

4. **Dependency:** "Could over-reliance on AI reduce our teaching insight?"
   - Discuss: AI is a tool; we still need to deeply understand student work

5. **Privacy:** "What student data is safe to input into AI?"
   - Rule: Anonymize; don't input sensitive information

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Feedback is too generic | Add more specific criteria and context to prompt |
| Tone is too harsh/soft | Specify tone adjustment in prompt |
| AI misses key issues | Review carefully; add missing points manually |
| Feedback is too long | Set word limit in prompt; edit down |
| Doesn't match assessment criteria | Include rubric/criteria explicitly in prompt |
| Sounds robotic | Personalization step is crucial; add human voice |

## Time-Saving Calculations

### Facilitator Can Share:

**Traditional Feedback:**
- 30 essays x 5 minutes each = 150 minutes (2.5 hours)

**With AI Assistance:**
- AI draft: 30 x 30 seconds = 15 minutes
- Personalize: 30 x 2 minutes = 60 minutes
- **Total: 75 minutes (1.25 hours)**
- **Time saved: 75 minutes (50%!)**

**Quality Check:**
- Is AI feedback better than rushed traditional feedback? Often yes.
- Is it better than ideal traditional feedback? Depends on personalization.
