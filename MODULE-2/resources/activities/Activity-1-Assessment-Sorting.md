# Activity 1: Assessment Type Sorting Challenge

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Format:** Interactive discussion and sorting
**Objective:** Understand where AI is most effective in the assessment landscape

---

## Activity Overview

This opening activity gets participants thinking critically about different types of assessment and where AI can genuinely help vs. where human judgment is essential.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this activity, participants will be able to:
- Identify different assessment types
- Evaluate where AI can assist with assessment
- Recognize the limitations of AI in assessment
- Make informed decisions about AI use in their context

---

## Materials Needed

### Digital Version
- Mentimeter or Padlet
- Pre-loaded assessment cards
- Shared screen

### Physical Version
- Printed assessment type cards (provided below)
- Three zones marked: "AI Excels" / "AI Assists" / "Human Essential"
- Blu-tack or magnetic tape
- Large chart paper

## Setup

### Facilitator Preparation
1. Prepare the three zones (digital columns or physical areas)
2. Have assessment cards ready to display/distribute
3. Prepare examples of each assessment type
4. Queue up discussion prompts

### Labels for Three Zones

**Zone 1: AI Excels**
"AI can do this quickly and effectively with minimal human input"

**Zone 2: AI Assists**
"AI can help, but significant human review/input needed"

**Zone 3: Human Essential**
"Human judgment is crucial; AI has limited role"

## Activity Instructions

### Part 1: Individual Thinking (2 mins)

**Instructions to Participants:**
1. Review the assessment types listed
2. Think about your own assessment practices
3. Consider: Which of these do you spend the most time on?
4. Note: Where would you most welcome AI assistance?

### Part 2: Collaborative Sorting (5 mins)

**Instructions:**

**For Physical Version:**
1. Invite participants to the front (or work in table groups)
2. Take turns placing cards in the three zones
3. Discuss placement if there's disagreement
4. It's okay if cards overlap zones

**For Digital Version:**
1. Use Padlet or Menti collaborative board
2. Participants drag cards to zones
3. Discussion in chat or verbal

**Facilitator Role:**
- Don't give "right answers" yet
- Ask: "Why did you put that there?"
- Encourage discussion: "Does anyone disagree?"
- Note interesting debates for debrief

### Part 3: Debrief & Discussion (3 mins)

**Facilitator-Led Discussion:**

**Reveal the "Expert" Categories:**

**AI Excels:**
- Multiple choice quiz generation
- Spelling test marking
- Grammar and punctuation check
- Exit ticket analysis (pattern spotting)
- Rubric creation (structure and criteria)
- Diagnostic misconception identification

**AI Assists (Review Required):**
- Essay feedback (AI drafts, you personalize)
- Math problem-solving feedback
- Lab report marking
- Reading comprehension questions
- Student self-reflection analysis
- Peer assessment criteria
- Creative writing evaluation (basic feedback)

**Human Essential:**
- Oral presentation assessment (nuance, presence)
- Portfolio holistic evaluation (requires contextual judgment)
- Practical skills observation (requires presence)
- Group work contribution evaluation (requires observation)
- Critical thinking assessment (needs complex reasoning)
- High-stakes exam marking (unless auto-marked MCQ)
- Project-based assessment (human judgment central)

**Discussion Prompts:**
- "Are you surprised by any of these placements?"
- "Where did our sorting differ from the expert categories?"
- "What makes something 'AI Excels' vs. 'Human Essential'?"
- "Does this change what you'll use AI for?"

**Key Insight to Share:**
"AI is a tool, not a magic solution. It excels at structure, patterns, and routine tasks. It assists with feedback and analysis. But human judgment, nuance, and relationship remain essential in complex assessment."

## Extension Ideas

### For Groups Who Finish Early
- Add 5 assessment types from their own practice
- Categorize them into the three zones
- Share one example with the whole group

### For Advanced Participants
- Discuss: "How might these categories shift as AI improves?"
- Debate: "Should high-stakes exams ever use AI?"
- Consider: "What ethical concerns does each zone raise?"

## Common Misconceptions to Address

### Misconception 1: "AI can grade anything"
**Reality:** AI can grade structured tasks. Nuanced, context-dependent assessment needs humans.

### Misconception 2: "Using AI for marking is cheating/lazy"
**Reality:** AI for drafting feedback or identifying patterns is efficiency, not laziness. You still review and personalize.

### Misconception 3: "AI will replace teachers in assessment"
**Reality:** AI handles routine tasks. Teachers remain essential for judgment, relationships, and complex evaluation.

### Misconception 4: "If AI can't do it perfectly, I shouldn't use it"
**Reality:** AI doesn't need to be perfect to be helpful. Drafting saves time even if you heavily edit.

## Reflection Questions

### For Participants to Consider:
1. Which assessment task takes you the most time currently?
2. Based on today's sorting, could AI help with that task?
3. What would you gain if AI handled some "AI Excels" tasks?
4. What assessment practices would you want to keep entirely human?
5. What's one AI assessment tool you'll try this week?

---

## Facilitator Debrief Notes

### After This Activity:

**If participants are...**
- **Enthusiastic:** Great! Channel that energy into the hands-on sections.
- **Skeptical:** Validate concerns. Emphasize "AI Assists" and "Human Essential" zones.
- **Confused:** Simplify to: "AI is good at patterns and structure, humans are good at nuance and context."
- **Concerned about ethics:** Note concerns, promise to address in ethics section.

**Key Takeaway to Reinforce:**
"By the end of today, you'll know how to use AI in the 'AI Excels' category and do it ethically."

## Success Criteria

**This activity is successful if:**
- ✅ Participants can name 2-3 assessment types where AI excels
- ✅ Participants can explain why human judgment is essential in some contexts
- ✅ Participants feel more confident about where to start with AI
- ✅ The tone is balanced: excited but realistic

---

## Time Management

### If Running Short on Time:
- Skip individual thinking, go straight to sorting (saves 2 mins)
- Reduce card set to 10 instead of 20 (saves 2 mins)
- Quick debrief without extended discussion (saves 2 mins)

### If You Have Extra Time:
- Add real examples from participants' practice
- Deeper ethics discussion
- Role-play scenarios: "When would you use AI for this assessment?"
