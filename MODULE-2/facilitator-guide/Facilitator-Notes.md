# Facilitator Guide: Module 2
## AI for Assessment & Feedback

**Welcome, Facilitator!** This guide will help you deliver an impactful 3-hour workshop on AI-powered assessment and feedback.

---

## Before You Begin

### Your Role
You're helping teachers solve one of their biggest pain points: assessment workload. By the end, participants should feel **empowered and equipped** to use AI ethically and effectively for assessment.

### Key Principles
1. **Address ethics head-on** - Don't avoid the hard questions
2. **Show real impact** - Use actual student work examples
3. **Balance efficiency with integrity** - AI speeds things up without cutting corners
4. **Practice over theory** - 60% hands-on time
5. **Model the process** - Show drafting AND personalizing

---

## Delivery Tips by Section

### Opening (0:00-0:15)

**Energy Level:** EMPATHETIC - Acknowledge the struggle

**Your Goals:**
- Connect with the real pain of assessment workload
- Build hope that AI can help sustainably
- Set ethical framework from the start

**Critical First Impressions:**
- Acknowledge: "Assessment is exhausting. I get it."
- Validate feelings: "Many teachers spend more time marking than teaching."
- Offer hope: "Today we find ways to work smarter."

**Common Pitfalls:**
- ❌ Being defensive about AI ethics
- ❌ Promising AI will solve everything
- ❌ Dismissing concerns as "resistance to change"
- ✅ Honest conversation about benefits AND limitations

**Read the Room:**
- If participants seem skeptical: Address ethics early
- If they seem overwhelmed: Emphasize time savings
- If they're eager: Channel energy into first activity

**Key Questions to Pose:**
- "How many hours on assessment per week?" (Get real numbers)
- "What would you do with 5 extra hours?" (Make it personal)
- "What stops you giving feedback you'd like to?" (Usually: time)

---

### Assessment Creation (0:15-0:45)

**Energy Level:** HIGH - Show the wow factor!

**Your Goals:**
- Install/access assessment tools
- Demonstrate 3-4 quick wins
- Get everyone creating assessments

**Demonstration Tips:**

**1. Have diverse content ready**
- Science article about climate change
- History document on suffragettes
- Math concept explanation
- Literary text excerpt
- Language learning passage

**2. Show the differentiation power**
- Same content, three levels
- Emphasize: "Differentiation without drowning"

**3. Narrate your choices**
- "I'm setting this to 'medium' because..."
- "I changed this question because..."
- "I'd use this for formative assessment because..."

**4. Address quality concerns immediately**
- Show a poor question
- Demonstrate editing it
- Say: "AI drafts, you refine"

**Common Questions & Responses:**

**Q: "Will this match my curriculum?"**
A: "You can specify curriculum standards. I'll show you..." (demonstrate)

**Q: "Aren't the questions too basic/hard?"**
A: "That's why you review and adjust. Let me show editing..." (demonstrate)

**Q: "Can students find these answers online?"**
A: "Yes, which is why we use it for formative, not summative high-stakes. Or we adapt."

**Activity Support Tips:**
- First 5 mins: Ensure everyone can access the tool
- Circulate constantly
- Help with prompt refinement: "Try adding 'misconceptions' to your prompt"
- Collect 2-3 excellent examples to showcase
- Extension: "Now create an exit ticket for today's session"

**Time Management:**
- If running behind: Skip demo 4, move to activity
- If ahead: Show tool comparison (Quizizz vs. Google Forms AI vs. Kahoot)

---

### Feedback Generation (0:55-1:30)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM - This is emotional for many teachers

**Your Goals:**
- Show feedback generation power
- Address "is this cheating?" concern
- Demonstrate personalization process

**Sensitive Topic Alert:**
Many teachers have guilt about feedback quality/quantity. Be empathetic.

**Demonstration Tips:**

**1. Use realistic student work**
- Not perfect or terrible - middle ground
- Show work with both strengths and areas to improve
- Multiple subjects

**2. Show the full process**
- AI draft (30 seconds)
- Your review (1 minute)
- Personalization (1-2 minutes)
- Final product (better than you'd write in 5 minutes)

**3. Demonstrate tone adjustment**
- Same work, encouraging tone
- Same work, constructively critical
- Same work, student with anxiety (gentler)
- Show how context matters

**4. Address the "soul" concern**
- Some will say: "Feedback needs the teacher's voice"
- Response: "Absolutely. AI drafts the structure, you add the soul."
- Demonstrate adding: "[Student name], I loved how you..." (personal touch)

**Common Concerns & Responses:**

**Q: "Can students tell it's AI?"**
A: "If you don't personalize, possibly. That's why step 2 is crucial. Let me show..." (demo before/after)

**Q: "What about handwritten feedback?"**
A: "AI can draft. You can write it out by hand if that's your style. Still saves thinking time."

**Q: "This feels inauthentic."**
A: "Think of it like a writing assistant. You wouldn't write an important email without re-reading. Same here."

**Q: "What if I disagree with AI's feedback?"**
A: "Perfect! Change it. You're the expert. AI suggests, you decide."

**Activity Support Tips:**
- Provide work samples for those who didn't bring any
- Encourage: "Don't aim for perfect, aim for useful"
- Model thinking aloud: "I'd change this part because I know this student..."
- Emphasize privacy: "Don't use real student names in the AI tool"
- Pair teachers from same subject to peer review feedback

**Extension for Early Finishers:**
- Create feedback for different student levels
- Generate parent-communication versions
- Write feedback for self/peer assessment

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- Someone copying AI feedback without review → Intervene gently
- Participants uncomfortable with ethics → Make time for discussion
- Technology barriers preventing participation → Pair them up

---

### Rubric Design (1:30-2:00)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM - Collaborative and practical

**Your Goals:**
- Create comprehensive rubrics quickly
- Show student-facing versions
- Connect to consistency and fairness

**Demonstration Tips:**

**1. Show a rubric nightmare**
- Complex assignment with vague criteria
- "What does 'good effort' mean?"
- AI creates specific descriptors

**2. Demonstrate iteration**
- Generate rubric
- Review: "This level is too vague"
- Regenerate with refinement
- Show the improvement

**3. Subject variety**
- Science practical rubric
- Essay rubric
- Math problem-solving rubric
- Art/DT project rubric
- PE performance rubric
- Oral presentation rubric

**4. Student-friendly versions**
- Turn rubric into "I can..." checklist
- Show how this enables self-assessment
- Demonstrate peer assessment version

**Common Questions & Responses:**

**Q: "Our school has a required rubric format."**
A: "Perfect. Use AI to generate content, then format it to your template. Still saves time."

**Q: "What about holistic assessment?"**
A: "Rubrics can be holistic too. AI can create descriptive levels without numerical points."

**Q: "How do I know it's covering everything?"**
A: "You check against your learning objectives. AI helps draft, you ensure coverage."

**Activity Support Tips:**
- Show rubric templates from different subjects
- Help align to assessment objectives
- Suggest: "Start with AI, then add your school-specific requirements"
- Encourage sharing between subjects: "PE rubric can inspire Art rubric structure"

**Time Saver Tip:**
If running behind, shorten this section:
- Do only 2 demos instead of 3
- Shorten activity to 10 mins
- Skip gallery walk, do quick shares

---

### Work Analysis (2:10-2:40)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH - This is powerful stuff!

**Your Goals:**
- Show pattern recognition capabilities
- Demonstrate actionable insights
- Emphasize teacher decision-making

**Demonstration Tips:**

**1. Use real-ish data**
- Not perfect dataset
- Show messy reality
- Include outliers

**2. Make insights visual**
- Create simple chart/table from AI analysis
- Show: "15 students struggle with X, 5 need extension on Y"
- Actionable groupings

**3. Connect to next steps**
- "AI found this pattern, so tomorrow I'll..."
- Show re-teaching plan
- Demonstrate differentiation grouping

**4. Show speed**
- "This analysis took 2 minutes. Manually? 30+ minutes."
- Time saved = time for teaching

**Common Questions & Responses:**

**Q: "I don't have time to input all this data."**
A: "Start small. One exit ticket. One quiz. Build the habit."

**Q: "My class is too small for patterns."**
A: "Even with 15 students, AI can spot trends you miss."

**Q: "What if AI's interpretation is wrong?"**
A: "Trust your professional judgment. AI suggests, you verify against your observations."

**Activity Support Tips:**
- Provide sample datasets for those without
- Help with anonymization
- Guide prompt writing: "Ask: 'What are the top 3 misconceptions?'"
- Encourage specificity: "Instead of 'analyze this,' say 'identify common errors in...'"

**Privacy Reminder:**
- Before this section, remind: "Remove all student names and identifiers"
- Show how to code students (Student A, Student B)
- Emphasize: Check tool privacy policies

**Extension Ideas:**
- Compare two class sets (same lesson, different years)
- Track individual student over multiple assessments
- Identify which questions discriminated well

---

### Ethics & Closing (2:40-3:00)

**Energy Level:** THOUGHTFUL - This matters

**Your Goals:**
- Address ethical concerns openly
- Provide clear guidelines
- Send teachers away confident and ethical

**Ethics Discussion Tips:**

**1. Create psychological safety**
- "All concerns are valid"
- "We're figuring this out together"
- "There aren't perfect answers yet"

**2. Use real scenarios**
- "A parent asks if you used AI for feedback. What do you say?"
- "A student's AI-generated quiz answer is better than the mark scheme. Now what?"
- "You find two students with identical AI-generated answers. Cheating?"

**3. Framework for decision-making**
- Ask: "Does this help or replace my teaching judgment?" (Help = good)
- Ask: "Am I being transparent?" (Yes = good)
- Ask: "Would I defend this to a parent/head/Ofsted?" (Yes = good)

**4. Acknowledge the gray areas**
- "Some things we don't know yet"
- "Policies are evolving"
- "Start conservative, learn as we go"

**Common Concerns & Responses:**

**Q: "If I use AI feedback, am I really teaching?"**
A: "You're teaching more efficiently. The relationship and human judgment remain yours."

**Q: "What if my school doesn't allow AI?"**
A: "Use today's learning to inform policy conversations. Show what's possible ethically."

**Q: "Parents will think I'm lazy."**
A: "Transparency matters. 'I use AI to draft feedback faster so I can give you more detailed insights.' Most parents will love that."

**Q: "Exam boards ban AI."**
A: "For exam *responses*, yes. For *teacher* assessment tools? Usually fine. Check specific policies."

**Action Planning Support:**
- Make it concrete: Not "use AI more," but "Create quiz for Topic 5 using Quizizz AI"
- Check feasibility: "Can you do this this week?"
- Address barriers: "What might stop you? How can you overcome it?"

**Closing Tone:**
- Celebratory: "Look what you created today!"
- Realistic: "It's a journey. Start small."
- Supportive: "We're here if you need help."
- Inspiring: "You can love teaching again without the assessment burden."

## Handling Difficult Situations

### Scenario: "This is just making teachers obsolete"

**Response:**
"I hear that fear. Let me ask: Has a calculator made mathematicians obsolete? Or did it free them to do more complex work? AI is a tool, not a replacement. Your expertise, relationships, and judgment? Irreplaceable."

### Scenario: "AI makes mistakes. What if I give wrong feedback?"

**Response:**
"Excellent point. That's exactly why you review everything. Think of AI like a teaching assistant - helpful but needs oversight. You wouldn't publish an assistant's work without checking. Same here."

### Scenario: "Students will use AI to cheat on assessments"

**Response:**
"They might try. That's why we: 1) Teach AI literacy, 2) Design assessments AI can't easily do, 3) Use AI for formative, not summative high-stakes, 4) Focus on process not just product. This is an assessment design challenge, which we're all navigating together."

### Scenario: Participant says this violates their professional integrity

**Response:**
"I respect that. Can we explore what feels uncomfortable? [Listen] ... What if you tried just one small thing - maybe rubric generation - and see how it feels? You can always choose not to use tools that don't align with your values."

### Scenario: Tech failure mid-demo

**Response:**
"And this is real life! Good thing we have backups. [Show screenshot/video] This is why you always preview AI outputs before using them. Tech fails, but having drafts ready helps."

### Scenario: Participant shares student-identifiable information with AI

**Response:**
[Gently, privately if possible] "Remember to anonymize student data. Let's pause and remove names before we continue. Privacy is crucial." [Then move on without shame]

## Time Management Strategies

### If Running Over Time

**Cut/Shorten:**
- Icebreaker discussion (cut 5 mins)
- Demo 4 in any section (cut 3-5 mins)
- Gallery walks (cut, do rapid shares instead)
- Ethics discussion (hit key points, share resources for reading)

**Don't Cut:**
- Hands-on activities (participants need practice)
- Ethics segment entirely (too important)
- Final action planning (they need takeaways)

### If Running Under Time

**Extend:**
- Ethics discussion (always more to explore)
- Extra demo: Google Classroom AI features
- Bonus: Reporting AI tools
- Gallery walk showcase
- Q&A session

## Differentiation for Participants

### By Experience Level
- **New to AI:** Provide step-by-step screenshots, pair with experienced
- **Familiar with AI:** Offer advanced prompts, extension challenges
- **AI skeptics:** Pair with enthusiastic peer, focus on ethics, emphasize choice

### By Subject
Have ready:
- **Science:** Lab report rubrics, practical assessment
- **Math:** Problem-solving feedback, misconception analysis
- **English:** Essay feedback, creative writing rubrics
- **Humanities:** Source analysis, extended writing
- **Languages:** Speaking rubrics, listening assessment
- **Arts:** Portfolio assessment, performance rubrics
- **PE:** Skills rubrics, performance feedback

### By Role
- **Classroom teachers:** Focus on direct application
- **SENCOs:** Highlight accessibility features
- **Leaders:** Emphasize school-wide policy
- **ECTs:** Provide extra scaffolding and support

## Quick Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Tool won't load | Use backup video, pivot to ChatGPT |
| Participant can't access tool | Pair them up, share screen from another device |
| Ethical objection halts session | Validate concern, table for ethics discussion, keep moving |
| Assessment created is poor quality | Perfect teachable moment: "This is why we review!" |
| Running way over time | Skip to ethics and action plan, share rest as resources |
| Participant disengaged | Check in privately at break, adjust examples to their subject |
| Group very skeptical | Lean into it: "Good! Critical thinking matters. Let's test these tools." |
| Tech confidence very low | Slow down, add more step-by-step, extend hands-on time |
