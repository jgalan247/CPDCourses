# Module 2: Session Plan
## AI for Assessment & Feedback

**Duration:** 3 hours (180 minutes)
**Format:** Interactive workshop
**Audience:** Teachers (all subjects, all levels)
**Prerequisite:** Module 1 or basic AI familiarity

---

## Session Overview

This session transforms teachers' assessment practices from time-consuming to time-efficient while maintaining quality and personalization. The workshop balances AI-powered efficiency with pedagogical best practices and ethical considerations.

### Session Philosophy: "Assess, Analyze, Adapt, Act"
- **Assess:** Create varied, quality assessments quickly
- **Analyze:** Use AI to identify patterns and gaps
- **Adapt:** Generate personalized feedback at scale
- **Act:** Implement ethical AI assessment practices

---

## Detailed Session Timeline

### SEGMENT 1: Welcome & Assessment Realities (0:00 - 0:15) - 15 minutes

#### 0:00-0:05 | Welcome & Reconnection (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Welcome participants warmly
- Brief recap: "In Module 1, we learned to create resources. Today, we assess them."
- Acknowledge the challenge: "Assessment and feedback take huge amounts of time"
- Set the tone: "Today we make assessment sustainable"

**Key Message:** "Quality feedback shouldn't mean teacher burnout."

#### 0:05-0:15 | The Assessment Challenge Discussion (10 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Pose the question: "How much time do you spend on assessment and feedback weekly?"
- Collect responses (show of hands or Mentimeter poll)
- Common answers: 5-15 hours per week
- Calculate: "That's 200-600 hours per year!"

**Interactive Activity:**
Create a collaborative list (Padlet/whiteboard):
- "What makes assessment time-consuming?"

**Expected Responses:**
- Creating varied question banks
- Writing individual feedback
- Grading written work
- Designing rubrics
- Identifying common errors
- Tracking progress

**Facilitator Summary:**
"Today's AI tools can help with ALL of these. Let's see how..."

**Transition:** "Let's start with the foundation of assessment: creating the actual tests and quizzes..."

---

### SEGMENT 2: AI-Powered Assessment Creation (0:15 - 0:45) - 30 minutes

#### 0:15-0:20 | Assessment Types & AI Capabilities (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Display assessment pyramid: Diagnostic → Formative → Summative
- Explain where AI excels:
  - ✅ Question generation
  - ✅ Multiple versions for differentiation
  - ✅ Auto-grading setup
  - ✅ Answer key creation
  - ⚠️ Creativity assessment (needs human judgment)
  - ⚠️ Oral assessment (AI assists, doesn't replace)

**Share the Slide:** "AI Assessment Strengths & Limitations"

#### 0:20-0:30 | Quizizz AI Demonstration (10 mins)
**Facilitator Actions - Live Demo:**

**Demo 1: AI-Generated Quiz from Topic (3 mins)**
1. Open Quizizz and click "Create"
2. Select "AI Quiz Generator"
3. Enter topic: "Photosynthesis for Year 8"
4. Set parameters:
   - Number of questions: 10
   - Difficulty: Medium
   - Question types: Mixed (MCQ, True/False, Fill-in)
5. Generate and show results
6. Highlight: automatic images, clear distractors, aligned to curriculum

**Demo 2: Quiz from Document/URL (3 mins)**
1. Upload a PDF or paste article URL
2. Show Quizizz extracting key concepts
3. Generate comprehension questions
4. Emphasize: "Turn any reading into an assessment"

**Demo 3: Differentiated Versions (2 mins)**
1. Take the same quiz
2. Click "Adapt"
3. Create easier version (Year 6)
4. Create harder version (GCSE)
5. Show side-by-side comparison

**Demo 4: Answer Explanations (2 mins)**
1. Show how AI generates explanations for each answer
2. Demonstrate editing for your context
3. Highlight: "Students learn from wrong answers too"

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"That's 3 differentiated quizzes with explanations in under 10 minutes."

#### 0:30-0:45 | Activity 2: Create Your Own Assessments (15 mins)
**Objective:** Every teacher creates at least 2 assessments

**Instructions:**
1. Choose your preferred tool (Quizizz AI, Magic School AI, or other)
2. Create assessments for:
   - **Challenge 1:** Formative assessment (5-10 questions) for upcoming topic
   - **Challenge 2:** Exit ticket (2-3 questions) for recent lesson
   - **Extension:** Create differentiated versions OR diagnostic pre-assessment

**Differentiation Options:**
- Beginner: Use templates provided
- Intermediate: Create from scratch
- Advanced: Create multi-level versions

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Circulate and provide support
- Help with question quality refinement
- Encourage subject variety
- Screenshot excellent examples

**Share Time (last 3 mins):**
- Quick poll: "What type did you create?"
- 2 volunteers share their assessment
- Facilitator highlights quality elements

**Transition:** "You can create assessments quickly. Now let's tackle the biggest time sink: feedback..."

### SEGMENT 3: AI for Feedback Generation (0:55 - 1:30) - 35 minutes

#### 0:55-1:00 | The Feedback Challenge (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Pose the scenario: "30 students submit essays. You want to give quality feedback."
- Traditional time: 5-10 minutes per essay = 2.5-5 hours
- The dilemma: Speed vs. Quality vs. Teacher wellbeing

**Discussion Prompts:**
- "What makes good feedback?" (Specific, actionable, timely, balanced)
- "What prevents us from giving it?" (Time!)

**Key Message:** "AI can draft feedback. You refine and personalize it."

#### 1:00-1:15 | AI Feedback Tools Demonstration (15 mins)
**Facilitator Actions - Multiple Demonstrations:**

**Demo 1: Magic School AI - Written Work Feedback (5 mins)**
1. Select "AI Feedback Generator"
2. Input assignment details: "Year 10 essay on Macbeth themes"
3. Paste sample student work (prepared in advance)
4. Set parameters:
   - Feedback tone: Encouraging
   - Focus areas: Thesis strength, evidence use, analysis depth
   - Student level: Middle ability
5. Generate feedback
6. Show: Strengths highlighted + Specific improvements + Next steps

**Demo 2: Bulk Feedback Generation (4 mins)**
1. Show spreadsheet approach
2. Student name, work summary, key issues
3. Use ChatGPT or Magic School with template:
   ```
   Create feedback for [Student Name] on [Assignment].
   Strengths: [List]
   Areas for growth: [List]
   Tone: [Encouraging/Constructive]
   Include: 2 specific strengths, 2 areas for improvement, 1 next step
   ```
4. Demonstrate batch processing
5. Emphasize: "Draft in 20 minutes, personalize in 40 = 60 mins total vs. 3+ hours"

**Demo 3: Subject-Specific Feedback (3 mins)**
**Maths:** Show feedback on problem-solving process
**Science:** Show feedback on practical write-up
**English:** Show feedback on creative writing
**Art:** Show feedback on design work

Point out: "AI understands subject-specific criteria"

**Demo 4: Differentiated Feedback (3 mins)**
1. Same piece of work
2. Generate feedback for:
   - EAL student (simpler vocabulary, visual prompts)
   - Student with dyslexia (focus on ideas not spelling)
   - Gifted student (extension challenges)
3. Show how tone and content adapt

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"You're still the teacher. AI drafts, you add the personal touch."

#### 1:15-1:30 | Activity 3: Generate Feedback Workshop (15 mins)
**Objective:** Create personalized feedback for real student work

**Setup:**
Provide options:
- **Option A:** Use own student work (anonymized)
- **Option B:** Use provided sample work (from resource pack)

**Instructions:**
1. Select 3-5 pieces of student work
2. Use AI to generate draft feedback
3. Personalize with:
   - Student name
   - Specific examples from their work
   - Personal knowledge (e.g., "building on your improvement from last week")
4. Save your feedback templates

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Provide sample work across subjects
- Model the "draft and personalize" approach
- Emphasize: check for accuracy
- Collect questions for ethics discussion later

**Share Time (last 3 mins):**
- Ask: "How long would this have taken traditionally vs. with AI?"
- Share time savings estimates
- 1-2 people share examples

**Transition:** "Feedback is crucial, but so is clarity about what you're assessing. Let's create rubrics..."

### SEGMENT 4: Rubric Design & Success Criteria (1:30 - 2:00) - 30 minutes

#### 1:30-1:35 | Why Rubrics Matter (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Brief discussion: "Hands up if you've spent ages creating a rubric"
- Benefits of clear rubrics:
  - Consistency in marking
  - Transparency for students
  - Faster grading
  - Student self-assessment enabled

**The AI Advantage:**
- Generate comprehensive rubrics in minutes
- Ensure all criteria covered
- Student-friendly language versions
- Subject-specific expertise built in

#### 1:35-1:45 | Rubric Creation Demonstrations (10 mins)
**Facilitator Actions - Live Demos:**

**Demo 1: Magic School AI Rubric Generator (4 mins)**
1. Select "Rubric Generator"
2. Assignment: "Year 9 Science: Design a fair test investigation"
3. Input parameters:
   - Type: Practical investigation
   - Point scale: 1-4
   - Criteria areas: Hypothesis, Method, Results, Analysis, Conclusion
4. Generate rubric
5. Show: Clear descriptors for each level
6. Demonstrate exporting to Google Docs/PDF

**Demo 2: ChatGPT Custom Rubric (3 mins)**
Use prompt:
```
Create a detailed rubric for a Year 7 History project on Roman Britain.
Assessment criteria: Research, Analysis, Presentation, Source Use
4 levels: Emerging, Developing, Secure, Advanced
Include student-friendly language
```
Show the output and formatting options

**Demo 3: Success Criteria Creation (3 mins)**
1. Show simplified version for younger students
2. Use "I can..." statements
3. Demo: Turn rubric into checklist
4. Student self-assessment version

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"Rubrics bring transparency. AI makes them quick to create and easy to customize."

#### 1:45-2:00 | Activity 4: Design Your Rubrics (15 mins)
**Objective:** Create 2 rubrics/success criteria for upcoming assessments

**Instructions:**
Choose TWO from:
- **Option 1:** Project rubric (extended assignment)
- **Option 2:** Essay rubric (written work)
- **Option 3:** Practical work rubric (Science, PE, Art, DT)
- **Option 4:** Presentation rubric (oral assessment)
- **Option 5:** Success criteria checklist (any assessment type)

**Process:**
1. Generate initial rubric with AI
2. Review and refine for your context
3. Create student-friendly version
4. Add any school-specific requirements

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Provide rubric templates
- Show examples from different subjects
- Help align to school assessment policies
- Encourage peer sharing

**Share Time (last 3 mins):**
- Gallery walk (physical or digital)
- Participants view 2-3 others' rubrics
- Note one idea to steal

**Transition:** "Now we have assessments, feedback, and rubrics. Let's use AI to analyze what students actually know..."

### SEGMENT 5: Analyzing Student Work with AI (2:10 - 2:40) - 30 minutes

#### 2:10-2:15 | The Power of Pattern Recognition (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Explain: "AI excels at finding patterns humans might miss"
- Use cases:
  - Common misconceptions across a class
  - Individual student progress trends
  - Areas where whole class struggled
  - Differentiation grouping

**Key Message:** "Data-informed teaching, not data-overwhelmed teaching."

#### 2:15-2:25 | Work Analysis Demonstrations (10 mins)
**Facilitator Actions - Live Demos:**

**Demo 1: Identifying Common Errors (3 mins)**
1. Show 10 student responses to same maths problem
2. Copy into ChatGPT with prompt:
   ```
   Analyze these 10 student responses to [problem].
   Identify:
   - Common misconceptions
   - Most frequent errors
   - What this tells us about understanding
   - Suggested re-teaching focus
   ```
3. Show AI analysis
4. Highlight actionable insights

**Demo 2: Progress Tracking (3 mins)**
1. Show student's work from 3 different time points
2. Prompt AI:
   ```
   Compare this student's work across 3 assessments.
   Track improvement in: [criteria]
   Identify: Strengths, Growth areas, Next steps
   ```
3. Show progress narrative generated
4. Use for reports or parent meetings

**Demo 3: Differentiation Grouping (2 mins)**
1. Input class assessment results summary
2. Ask AI to suggest groupings for differentiated instruction
3. Show: Gap focus groups, Extension groups, Peer support pairs
4. Emphasize: AI suggests, teacher decides

**Demo 4: Exit Ticket Analysis (2 mins)**
1. Show 25 exit ticket responses
2. Quick AI analysis:
   - % who understood concept
   - Common confusions
   - Ready for next lesson or need review?
3. Immediate teaching adjustment

**Facilitator Emphasis:**
"This is formative assessment on steroids - faster insights, better decisions."

#### 2:25-2:40 | Activity 5: Real Work Analysis (15 mins)
**Objective:** Analyze actual student work to inform teaching

**Setup:**
Participants use:
- **Option A:** Their own class data/work samples
- **Option B:** Provided dataset (sample class exit tickets, quiz responses)

**Instructions:**
1. Select a recent assessment or set of student responses
2. Use AI to analyze for:
   - Common misconceptions
   - Individual student needs
   - Class-wide gaps
   - Next teaching steps
3. Create an action plan based on findings

**Guiding Questions:**
- What patterns emerge?
- What surprised you?
- How will this change your next lesson?
- Which students need targeted support?

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Provide analysis prompt templates
- Help with data privacy (anonymize student work)
- Support interpretation of AI findings
- Encourage critical evaluation: "Does this match your observations?"

**Share Time (last 3 mins):**
- Pair-share: Discuss one insight
- Whole group: 2 volunteers share discoveries
- Facilitator highlights: "Using data without drowning in it"

**Transition:** "We've explored powerful tools. Now let's talk about using them ethically..."

### SEGMENT 6: Ethics, Sharing & Action Planning (2:40 - 3:00) - 20 minutes

#### 2:40-2:50 | AI Assessment Ethics Discussion (10 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
Present ethical considerations and facilitate discussion

**Key Ethical Issues:**

**1. Academic Integrity (2 mins)**
- Question: "Can students use AI on assessments?"
- Considerations:
  - What are we actually assessing?
  - Is AI use disclosed?
  - Different rules for different assessment types
- Stance: Be explicit in assignment guidelines

**2. Bias & Fairness (2 mins)**
- AI can perpetuate biases
- Examples: Language bias for EAL, cultural assumptions
- Mitigation: Human review, diverse examples, student input
- Always check AI feedback for fairness

**3. Data Privacy (2 mins)**
- Never input identifiable student data
- Use anonymized samples
- Check tool privacy policies
- School/district policies on AI use

**4. Teacher Judgment (2 mins)**
- AI assists, doesn't replace
- Professional expertise is essential
- AI for drafting, teacher for deciding
- High-stakes assessment needs human oversight

**5. Transparency (2 mins)**
- Tell students when AI is used
- Explain how it helps (faster feedback, more resources)
- Model ethical AI use
- Involve students in discussing AI in assessment

**Interactive Element:**
Quick poll or show of hands:
- "Will you disclose AI use to students?" (Yes/No/Sometimes)
- "What's your biggest ethical concern?" (Collect responses)

**Facilitator Summary:**
"Ethics aren't obstacles - they're guardrails for doing this well."

#### 2:50-2:55 | Showcase & Celebration (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**

**Quick Reflection:**
- "What did you create today?" (List on screen)
  - Assessments
  - Feedback
  - Rubrics
  - Analysis insights

**Celebration:**
- Calculate collective time saved: "If everyone saved 2 hours this week, that's [X] hours for this group!"
- Share best examples (with permission)
- Highlight creativity and adaptation

**Follow-Up Resources Shared:**
- All handouts and templates
- Video recordings of demos
- Prompt library
- Community group link
- Monthly tips subscription

#### 2:55-3:00 | Action Planning & Close (5 mins)
**Facilitator Actions:**
- Display/distribute Action Plan Template
- Everyone completes:
  1. **This week I will:** [One immediate action]
  2. **This month I will:** [One bigger implementation]
  3. **I need support with:** [One barrier or question]

**Quick Sharing:**
- Popcorn style: volunteers shout out their "This week" action

**Post-Workshop Evaluation:**

**Final Message:**
"Assessment doesn't have to be exhausting. Use AI to work smarter, not harder - and save your energy for the teaching you love."

**Thanks and Q&A:**
- Open floor for final questions
- Provide contact for ongoing support

## Facilitator Quick Reference

### Time Management Tips
- Set visible timer for activities
- Give 2-minute and 1-minute warnings
- Ethics discussion can expand - watch the clock
- Have extension activities ready
- Can shorten final sharing if needed

### Energy Management
- Start with engaging discussion (assessment pain points)
- Alternate demos and hands-on
- Breaks are crucial - honor them
- Celebrate wins frequently
- Music during independent work (optional)

### Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Tool not loading | Have screenshots/videos as backup |
| Participant skeptical about AI accuracy | Show human review process, emphasize AI as draft |
| "This feels like cheating" | Reframe: "Using a calculator isn't cheating. Neither is this." |
| Ethical concerns derail session | Acknowledge, note for ethics segment, keep moving |
| Mixed tech skills | Pair strong with struggling participants |
| Runs over time | Skip gallery walk, do quick popcorn sharing |
| Internet fails | Use pre-prepared examples, focus on principles |

### Differentiation for Participants
- Subject-specific examples ready (Math, English, Science, Humanities, Arts, Languages)
- Beginner: Provide templates and step-by-step
- Intermediate: Create from scratch with support
- Advanced: Multi-level versions, custom prompts
- Pair experienced with newcomers

### Response to Common Questions

**Q: "Will this replace teachers?"**
A: "No. It replaces tedious tasks, not teaching expertise. You're still essential."

**Q: "Is it accurate?"**
A: "It's a draft. Always review. Just like spell-check - helpful but not perfect."

**Q: "What about exam boards/standards?"**
A: "Use AI for internal assessment. Check exam board AI policies for high-stakes."

**Q: "Can students tell it's AI feedback?"**
A: "If you personalize it, no. That's why human touch is crucial."

**Q: "What if my school bans AI?"**
A: "Start conversations about policy. Show ethical use. Many schools are writing policies now."
