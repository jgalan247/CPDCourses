✏️ Workshop Activities

Activity 1: AI Icebreaker Challenge 10 minutes

Your Challenge

Work with a partner to use AI to create the most creative icebreaker question for a Year 7 class (or adapt to your year group).

Instructions

  1. Open an AI tool: Go to ChatGPT (free version works)
  2. Enter a prompt: Use one of the prompts below or create your own
  3. Review and adapt: Choose the best response and personalize it
  4. Be ready to share! We'll hear from several pairs

Try These Prompts

Basic Prompt:

Create a creative icebreaker question for a Year 7 class on their first day.
Make it fun, safe, and engaging.

Advanced Prompt:

Create an unusual and creative icebreaker question for Year 7 students.
The question should:
- Be inclusive (no one feels left out)
- Spark interesting conversations
- Reveal something unique about each person
- Avoid common questions like "favorite color"
Give me 3 options.

Subject-Specific Prompt:

Create a creative icebreaker question for Year 9 Science class that relates
to science but is fun and non-intimidating. Include a follow-up activity idea.
You just created a classroom resource in 5 minutes! This is what AI can do - now imagine applying this to everything you teach.

Activity 2: Brisk AI Quick Wins 15 minutes

Your Mission

Complete at least 3 challenges in 15 minutes. Create resources you'll actually use!

Installation First!

  1. Open Chrome browser
  2. Go to Chrome Web Store
  3. Search for "Brisk Teaching"
  4. Click "Add to Chrome"
  5. Look for the Brisk icon in your toolbar 🚀

Challenge 1: Instant Quiz Creation (3 mins)

What you'll do: Turn any online article into a quiz

  1. Find an article related to your subject (BBC News, National Geographic, etc.)
  2. Click the Brisk icon in your browser toolbar
  3. Select "Create Quiz"
  4. Adjust settings: number of questions (5-10), question types, difficulty level
  5. Click Generate
  6. Review and save your quiz!

💡 Tip: Select specific text in the article first for more focused quizzes

Challenge 2: Differentiate Text (3 mins)

What you'll do: Adapt the same article for different reading levels

  1. Stay on the same article from Challenge 1
  2. Click the Brisk icon
  3. Select "Change Reading Level"
  4. Choose a target:
    • Simplify for struggling readers (KS2 level)
    • Increase complexity for advanced readers (A-Level)
  5. Click Generate and compare versions
  6. Download both versions

💡 Perfect for mixed-ability classes and EAL students!

Challenge 3: Learning Objectives (3 mins)

What you'll do: Generate SMART learning objectives for your next lesson

  1. Click the Brisk icon (from any page)
  2. Select "Create Learning Objectives"
  3. Enter your lesson details:
    • Topic: (e.g., "The Water Cycle")
    • Year group: (e.g., "Year 5")
    • Subject: (e.g., "Science")
    • Duration: (e.g., "60 minutes")
  4. Click Generate
  5. Review - objectives should be Specific, Measurable, Action-oriented
  6. Copy to your lesson plan

Bonus Challenges (If you finish early)

  • 📧 Email Template: Brisk > "Email" - Create parent communication about field trips
  • 💭 Discussion Questions: Generate 5-8 open-ended questions from an article
  • 📊 Presentation Slides: Create full Google Slides for your next lesson
  • 🎯 Exit Ticket: Quick formative assessment questions

Activity 4: Differentiation Workshop 10 minutes

Your Challenge

Create differentiated versions of one resource for at least TWO different learner groups.

Steps

  1. Choose a lesson or resource you currently teach
  2. Select at least 2 learner groups from the list below
  3. Use Brisk AI or Magic School AI to create adapted versions
  4. Use the prompt templates provided

Differentiation Prompts for Different Learner Groups

♿ For Autistic Learners:

Adapt this worksheet for an autistic Year 6 student. Include:
- Visual supports and icons
- Clear, literal language (no idioms)
- Reduced visual clutter
- Step-by-step instructions with numbers
- Visual schedule of tasks
- Predictable structure

📖 For Dyslexic Learners:

Rewrite this text for a dyslexic learner. Include:
- Dyslexia-friendly font (Comic Sans or OpenDyslexic)
- Increased line spacing (1.5 or 2.0)
- Text broken into small chunks
- Key words highlighted in bold
- Simplified sentence structure
- Visual aids where possible

🌍 For EAL (English as Additional Language) Students:

Adapt this lesson for EAL students with intermediate English. Include:
- Simplified vocabulary
- Visual glossary of key terms
- Sentence stems for responses
- Cultural context explanations
- Translation support for key concepts
- More visual aids

🎯 For Students with ADHD:

Redesign this lesson for students with ADHD. Include:
- Shorter activity bursts (5-10 minute chunks)
- Clear timers and time boundaries
- Built-in movement breaks
- Visual checklists for progress
- Gamification elements
- Minimal distractions on page

🌟 For Gifted & Talented Students:

Extend this task for gifted and talented students. Include:
- Open-ended challenges
- Cross-curricular connections
- Research and investigation opportunities
- Creative application tasks
- Higher-order thinking questions (analyze, evaluate, create)
- Independent project options
Remember: AI doesn't lower standards - it provides different access points to the same high-quality learning!

Activity 5: Create Your Own Resources 30 minutes

The Final Challenge

Create 1-3 resources you'll use THIS WEEK in your actual teaching!

Choose Your Project

Option 1: The Full Lesson

  • Complete lesson plan for next week

Key Ethical Principles

  • ✅ Always review AI-generated content for accuracy
  • ✅ Never share student data with AI tools
  • ✅ Adapt AI content to your class context - don't use as-is
  • ✅ Be transparent with students when using AI
  • ✅ You're the expert - AI is the assistant
  • ⚠️ Check facts, especially in Science, History, and current events
AI is a tool, not a replacement. You are the teacher - AI just saves you time on the routine stuff so you can focus on what matters: teaching!
