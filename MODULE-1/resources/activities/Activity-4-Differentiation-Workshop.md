# Activity 4: Differentiation Workshop

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Format:** Individual creation with shared examples
**Objective:** Create differentiated versions of resources for diverse learners (SEND, EAL, G&T)

---

## Overview

This focused workshop demonstrates AI's superpower: creating multiple versions of content efficiently. Participants will adapt one resource for at least two different learning needs, experiencing how AI makes inclusive teaching manageable.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this activity, participants will be able to:
- Create adapted resources for autistic learners
- Create dyslexia-friendly versions of texts
- Support EAL students with appropriate modifications
- Develop ADHD-friendly task structures
- Extend activities for gifted and talented students
- Use effective prompts for differentiation
- Understand AI's role in inclusive education

---

## Why This Matters

**The Challenge:**
- 1 in 6 students have some form of SEND
- Teachers spend 5-10+ hours per week differentiating
- Quality differentiation often gets skipped due to time
- One-size-fits-all teaching excludes many learners

**The AI Solution:**
- Create 5 versions as quickly as 1 version
- Research-based strategies implemented automatically
- Consistent quality across all versions
- More time for actual teaching and relationship-building

## Activity Structure

### Part 1: Understanding Differentiation with AI (3 minutes)

**Facilitator Script:**
> "Differentiation is essential but exhausting. Creating 3-4 versions of every lesson takes hours. But AI doesn't get tired. Let me show you some examples of what's possible..."

**Show 2-3 examples:**
1. Standard worksheet → Autism-friendly version (visual supports, literal language, reduced clutter)
2. Dense text → Dyslexia-friendly version (font, spacing, chunking, highlighting)
3. Basic task → Gifted & Talented extension (open-ended, cross-curricular, higher-order thinking)

**Key Message:**
"Notice: We're not lowering standards. We're providing access points. Every student can succeed."

### Part 2: Choose Your Students (1 minute)

**Facilitator Script:**
> "Think about your class. Who needs support? Choose at least TWO groups from the list in your worksheet. You'll create adapted versions for both. Ready?"

### Part 3: Creation Time (5 minutes)

**Participants create differentiated versions**

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Circulate quickly
- Provide prompt templates as needed
- Celebrate progress: "That's brilliant!"
- Screenshot examples (with permission)
- Keep energy high

### Part 4: Quick Share (1 minute)

**Facilitator Actions:**
- Ask 2-3 people to briefly show their adapted resources
- Highlight the speed: "How long would this normally take?"
- Emphasize practical use: "You'll use this tomorrow!"

## Step 2: Choose Your Learner Groups (Pick at least 2)

### GROUP 1: 🧩 Autistic Learners

**Common Needs:**
- Clear, literal language (no idioms)
- Visual supports and structure
- Reduced sensory overload
- Explicit step-by-step instructions
- Predictability and routine
- Concrete examples

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Adapt [this lesson/text/activity] for an autistic [year] student.
Include:
- Visual supports and diagrams
- Clear, literal language (no metaphors or idioms)
- Step-by-step instructions
- Reduced visual clutter
- Clear structure with beginning, middle, end
- Concrete examples rather than abstract concepts
```

**Specific Adaptations AI Will Make:**
- Add visual schedules
- Break tasks into numbered steps
- Use bullet points instead of paragraphs
- Add processing time reminders
- Include sensory break suggestions

---

### GROUP 2: 📖 Dyslexic Learners

**Common Needs:**
- Dyslexia-friendly formatting
- Reduced cognitive load
- Clear text structure
- Key information highlighted
- Multi-sensory approaches
- Chunked information

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Rewrite [this text/activity] for a dyslexic learner in [year].
Use:
- Dyslexia-friendly font and formatting
- Increased line spacing (1.5 or double)
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- Bullet points for lists
- Key vocabulary highlighted
- Chunked information with clear headings
Maintain the same content and learning objectives.
```

**Specific Adaptations AI Will Make:**
- Suggest OpenDyslexic font or similar
- Increase spacing between words/lines
- Break long paragraphs into chunks
- Bold key terms
- Add visual diagrams to support text
- Simplify sentence structure

---

### GROUP 3: 🌍 EAL (English as Additional Language) Students

**Common Needs:**
- Simplified vocabulary
- Visual support
- Sentence stems and frames
- Glossary of key terms
- Cultural context explanation
- Translation support

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Adapt [this resource] for EAL students with intermediate English in [year].
Include:
- Simplified vocabulary (replace complex words)
- Visual supports and diagrams
- Sentence stems to support writing
- Glossary of key subject terms with simple definitions
- Explanation of any cultural references
- Clear, short sentences
Maintain academic rigor.
```

**Specific Adaptations AI Will Make:**
- Replace academic vocabulary with simpler alternatives
- Add picture support
- Provide sentence stems: "I think that...", "This shows..."
- Create visual glossary
- Explain idioms and cultural references
- Suggest translation of key terms

---

### GROUP 4: 🎯 Students with ADHD

**Common Needs:**
- Shorter task chunks
- Movement breaks
- Visual timers
- Clear, immediate goals
- Gamification
- Reduced distractions

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Redesign [this lesson/activity] for students with ADHD in [year].
Include:
- Short, focused tasks (5-10 minute chunks)
- Movement breaks between sections
- Clear time boundaries for each task
- Visual progress tracking (checklists)
- Gamification elements (points, levels, challenges)
- Reduced visual distractions
- Immediate, concrete goals
```

**Specific Adaptations AI Will Make:**
- Break long tasks into 3-5 shorter tasks
- Add timer suggestions (use 10-minute timer)
- Include movement break prompts
- Add progress checklists
- Suggest reward systems
- Create clear visual boundaries

---

### GROUP 5: ⭐ Gifted & Talented Students

**Common Needs:**
- Challenge and extension
- Open-ended opportunities
- Independent research
- Cross-curricular links
- Creative application
- Higher-order thinking

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Extend [this task/lesson] for gifted and talented [year] students.
Add:
- Open-ended, challenging questions
- Independent research opportunities
- Cross-curricular links to [subjects]
- Creative application tasks
- Higher-order thinking (analysis, evaluation, creation)
- Choice and autonomy
Maintain connection to the original learning objectives.
```

**Specific Adaptations AI Will Make:**
- Add "What if...?" questions
- Suggest research extensions
- Create links to other subjects
- Propose creative projects
- Include philosophical questions
- Offer choice menus

---

### GROUP 6: 👂👁️ Visual or Hearing Impairments

**Common Needs:**
- Accessible formats
- Alternative sensory input
- Clear descriptions
- Assistive technology compatibility
- Large print or audio options

**Your Prompt Template:**
```
Adapt [this resource] for a student with [visual/hearing] impairment in [year].
Ensure:
- [For visual] Audio descriptions, tactile elements, large print
- [For hearing] Visual captions, written instructions, visual alerts
- Screen reader compatible formatting
- Clear, accessible document structure
- Alternative formats provided
```

## Step 3: Create Your Differentiated Versions

**Instructions:**
1. Open Brisk AI (if adapting text) OR Magic School AI (if adapting lesson)
2. Have your base resource ready (text, lesson plan, worksheet)
3. Use the prompt templates above
4. Fill in the blanks:
   - [this lesson/text/activity] = YOUR resource
   - [year] = YOUR year group
   - [subjects] = YOUR subject areas
5. Click generate
6. Review and adapt
7. Create for at least 2 different groups
8. Save all versions!

**Example in Action:**

**Original:** "Analyse the causes of World War I and evaluate which was most significant."

**For Autistic Learners:**
```
Step-by-step activity: Causes of World War I

Step 1: Read the information about each cause (5 minutes)
Step 2: List the causes in your notebook (use bullet points)
Step 3: For each cause, write one fact
Step 4: Choose which cause you think is most important
Step 5: Write two reasons why you chose that cause

[Visual diagram showing the causes as separate boxes with icons]
```

**For Dyslexic Learners:**
```
Causes of World War I - Investigation

Cause 1: Alliances
Countries made agreements to help each other.
• This meant many countries joined the war.

Cause 2: Imperialism
Countries wanted to control more land.
• This created competition and tension.

[etc. - short paragraphs, clear headings, bullet points]

Your Task:
Which cause was most important?
Write your answer: I think _______ was most important because...
```

**For Gifted & Talented:**
```
World War I Extended Investigation

Core: Analyse the causes and evaluate significance

Extensions:
1. Compare WWI causes to another major conflict - what patterns emerge?
2. If you could remove ONE cause, which would prevent the war? Defend your choice.
3. Research: How do modern historians debate these causes differently?
4. Create: Design a visual model showing the interconnection of causes

Choose 2-3 extensions. Present your findings creatively.
```

### During Creation Time: What to Do

- Circulate systematically
- Ask to see what they've created

- Remind to save
- Identify people to share
- Give countdown: "One more minute!"

### Common Support Needs

**"I don't know which group to choose"**
→ "Think about your class. Who struggles most? Start there."

**"The output is too generic"**
→ "Add more detail to your prompt. Describe the specific task."

**"Is this actually helpful for [need]?"**
→ "Great question! Compare to best practice guides. Usually yes, but always adapt."

**"I'm overwhelmed by choices"**
→ "Just do two: dyslexia and one other. That's enough."

## Common Questions & Answers

**Q: "Shouldn't I know how to do this without AI?"**
A: "You DO know! AI implements what YOU know, faster. It's like using a calculator - you understand maths, the calculator speeds it up."

**Q: "Is AI qualified to make SEND recommendations?"**
A: "AI uses research-based strategies. But YOU still review and adapt. You're the expert on YOUR students."

**Q: "Won't this make resources too easy?"**
A: "We're providing access, not lowering standards. Same objectives, different routes."

**Q: "What about students with multiple needs?"**
A: "Excellent point! You can combine strategies. E.g., 'Adapt for a dyslexic EAL student.'"

**Q: "Should I show students different versions?"**
A: "Complex question! Some teachers differentiate invisibly, others celebrate different pathways. Your choice."

**Q: "Can I use these for EHCPs or IEPs?"**
A: "AI-generated strategies can INFORM your plans. Always add your professional judgment and student-specific context."

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Output is too simple | Specify: "maintain academic rigor" |
| Not enough specific strategies | Add: "give 10 specific examples" |
| Doesn't understand the need | Try different phrasing or different AI tool |
| Too many changes | Ask for: "minimal necessary adaptations" |
| Participant disagrees with strategies | Great! That's critical thinking. Discuss why. |
| Finishes very quickly | Perfect! Try another group, or extend |
