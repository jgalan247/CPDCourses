# Facilitator Guide: Module 1
## AI Tools for Daily Tasks & Resource Creation

**Welcome, Facilitator!** This guide will help you deliver an engaging, practical, and transformative 3-hour workshop.

---

## Before You Begin

### Your Role
You're not just presenting information - you're guiding teachers through a hands-on transformation. By the end, participants should feel **confident and excited** about using AI in their teaching.

###Key Principles
1. **Show, don't just tell** - Live demonstrations are crucial
2. **Practice over theory** - 60% hands-on time
3. **Celebrate small wins** - Every success matters
4. **Create safety** - "You can't break AI!"
5. **Focus on practical** - Everything must be usable Monday morning

## Delivery Tips by Section

### Opening (0:00-0:20)

**Energy Level:** HIGH - Set the tone!

**Your Goals:**
- Make everyone feel welcome
- Build excitement
- Get them using AI immediately
- Gauge experience levels

**Critical First Impressions:**
- Smile, be enthusiastic
- Acknowledge any AI skepticism: "It's OK to be skeptical - let's see what it can do"
- Emphasize: "This is a DOING workshop"

**Common Pitfalls:**
- ❌ Talking too long at the start
- ❌ Not addressing concerns
- ❌ Boring icebreaker
- ✅ Jump straight into Activity 1 after brief intro

**Read the Room:**
- If participants seem anxious: slow down, add reassurance
- If they seem eager: pick up the pace
- If mixed: address both: "Some of you are AI experts, some haven't used it - both are perfect!"

---

### Brisk AI Showcase (0:20-0:50)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH - Exciting demos!

**Your Goals:**
- Install Brisk on all computers
- Demonstrate 5 quick wins
- Get everyone creating with Brisk

**Demonstration Tips:**
**1. Have backup content ready**
   - 3-4 articles pre-opened in tabs
   - Different subjects represented

**2. Narrate as you demonstrate**
   - "I'm clicking the Brisk icon..."
   - "Now I'm selecting Create Quiz..."
   - "Watch this - 10 seconds..."

**3. Show real-time creation**
   - Don't use pre-made examples for the demo
   - Let them see it generate live
   - If it fails, acknowledge and retry: "Sometimes it needs a second - that's normal"

**4. Point out key features**
   - "See how you can adjust difficulty?"
   - "Notice it includes answer key automatically"

**Common Issues:**
| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Brisk won't install | IT restrictions - have participants pair up with someone successful |
| Slow internet | Use prepared examples, show process |
| "It's not working for me" | Individual troubleshooting during activity time, not during demo |

**Activity Management (0:35-0:50):**
- Circulate actively
- Help individuals without stopping the whole group
- Screenshot great examples
- Keep energy up: "You're doing brilliantly!"
- Time warnings: "5 minutes left!"

---

### Break 1 (0:50-1:00)

**Use this time to:**
- Check in with struggling participants
- Queue up Magic School demos
- Troubleshoot any tech issues
- Hydrate and prepare for next section

**Pro tip:** Take a photo of the room/participants working (if allowed) - shows engagement for future marketing/reports

---

### Magic School AI Showcase (1:00-1:30)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM - Focused demonstration

**Your Goals:**
- Show the difference between Brisk and Magic School
- Demonstrate comprehensive tools
- Support individual exploration

**Positioning is Key:**
"Brisk is quick coffee - Magic School is a full meal. Both are valuable at different times."

**Demonstration Strategy:**
Choose demos relevant to your audience:
- **Primary teachers:** Focus on simple lesson plans, differentiation
- **Secondary teachers:** Show assessments, report comments
- **Mixed:** Show variety, let them explore what's relevant

**Activity Management (1:20-1:30):**
- Let participants work independently
- This is quieter than Brisk activity - that's fine
- Support individuals who need help
- Don't worry if not everyone completes - quality over speed

**Common Questions:**
"Is this better than Brisk?"
→ "Not better - different! Use both for different needs."

"Can I really use these lesson plans?"
→ "As starting points, absolutely! You'll adapt them to YOUR students."

---

### Differentiation Deep Dive (1:30-2:00)

**Energy Level:** MEDIUM - Important content

**Your Goals:**
- Show AI's power for inclusion
- Demonstrate specific adaptations
- Create practical differentiated resources

**Presentation Tips:**
**1. Lead with empathy**
   "Differentiation is exhausting. You want to do it, but there aren't enough hours..."

**2. Show concrete examples**
   - Use the before/after examples from the guides
   - Choose examples relevant to your audience's subjects

**3. Emphasize:**
   - We're not lowering standards
   - We're providing access
   - Same objectives, different routes

**Personal Stories (if you have them):**
Share your own differentiation challenges - builds connection

**Activity Management (1:50-2:00):**
- This is shorter (10 mins) - keep pace quick
- Encourage participants to create for students they actually teach
- Celebrate specific adaptations: "That's brilliant for your student!"

---

### Break 2 (2:00-2:10)

**Use this time to:**
- Prepare for final creation session
- Set up sharing platform if using digital showcase
- Address any lingering questions
- Give yourself a mental break!

---

### Creation Time (2:10-2:40)

**Energy Level:** CALM FOCUS - Deep work time

**Your Goals:**
- Every participant creates something they'll use
- Support without interrupting
- Maintain productive atmosphere

**Setting the Tone:**
"This is YOUR time. Create what YOU need. Take your time and make it good."

**Your Role:**
- Quiet presence
- Available but not intrusive
- Individual support as needed
- Respect the working atmosphere

**Optional: Play Music**
- Soft instrumental/ambient
- Volume low - background only
- Spotify: search "study music" or "focus music"

**Time Management:**
| Time Remaining | Action |
|----------------|---------|
| 15 mins | "You have 15 minutes left" |
| 10 mins | "10 minutes - you're doing great" |
| 5 mins | "5 minutes - start saving your work" |
| 2 mins | "2 minutes - ensure everything is saved" |
| 0 mins | "Time to share! Make sure you've saved." |

**Supporting Different Needs:**
- **Overwhelmed:** "Just create one simple lesson plan. That's enough."
- **Racing ahead:** "Brilliant! Now refine it or help a neighbor."
- **Perfectionist:** "Remember - done is better than perfect. You can improve later."
- **Stuck:** Sit with them, choose together, start with them

---

### Sharing & Close (2:40-3:00)

**Energy Level:** HIGH - Celebration!

**Your Goals:**
- Celebrate achievements
- Share learning
- Provide next steps
- End on inspiration

**Showcase Options:**

**Option A: Gallery Walk (Face-to-Face)**
1. "Put one resource on your desk"
2. "Walk around for 5 minutes - leave positive post-its"
3. "What impressed you?"
4. Group discussion of highlights

**Option B: Digital Showcase (Online/Hybrid)**
1. Share Padlet/Jamboard link
2. "Upload screenshot + brief description (3 mins)"
3. Browse together (5 mins)
4. Discuss highlights

**Option C: Rapid Shares**
1. Ask for 6-8 volunteers
2. 45 seconds each to share
3. Applause after each
4. Thank all sharers

**Closing Message - CRITICAL:**
This is your last chance to inspire ongoing use. Include:

1. **Recap the transformation:**
   "You came in [time] ago. Now you have [X] resources created, two powerful tools installed, and skills that will save you hours every week."

2. **Emphasize ongoing learning:**
   "This is day one. Keep experimenting, keep learning."

3. **Address ethics:**
   "Remember: AI is a tool, YOU are the teacher. Always review, fact-check, personalize."

4. **Provide next steps:**
   - Email with all resources (sent within 24 hours)
   - Community link for ongoing support
   - Follow-up session details (if applicable)

5. **End with inspiration:**
   "You now have a superpower. Use it to save time, reduce stress, and do what you do best - teach amazing lessons. Thank you!"

**Post-Workshop Survey:**
"Please complete the 2-minute survey - your feedback shapes future sessions."

## Handling Difficult Situations

### Resistant Participant
**Signs:** Arms crossed, not engaging, negative comments

**Strategy:**
1. Acknowledge: "I hear your concern"
2. Don't argue: "Try it and see what you think"
3. Pair with enthusiastic person
4. Individual check-in during break
5. Ask: "What would make this useful for YOU?"

**Remember:** You won't convert everyone. That's OK.

---

### Technical Meltdown
**Scenario:** Internet fails / tools don't work

**Backup Plan:**
1. Stay calm: "Technology! Let's use our backup plan"
2. Show pre-recorded demos OR pre-created examples
3. Focus on the concepts and principles
4. Encourage practice at home
5. Offer follow-up support session

---

### Running Over Time
**If you're 10+ minutes behind:**

**What to cut:**
- Gallery walk → Quick shares instead
- Extension activities
- Some example demonstrations

**What NOT to cut:**
- Hands-on practice time
- Creation time (Activity 5)
- Key demonstrations
- Closing message

**Prevention:**
- Set timers for yourself
- Have a clock visible
- Build in 5-min buffer
- Practice timing beforehand

---

### Participant Shows You Up
**Scenario:** "I already know all this" OR they're much more advanced

**Strategy:**
1. Celebrate: "Brilliant! Can I ask you to help others?"
2. Challenge: "Create something complex - push the tools to their limits"
3. Learn: "Show us what you've created - we'd love to see"

**Remember:** This is an opportunity, not a threat

---

### Unexpected Questions

**"Will AI replace teachers?"**
→ "No. AI replaces tasks, not relationships. Can AI comfort an upset child? Build confidence? Inspire? That's all you."

**"Is this ethical?"**
→ "Great question! AI is a tool, like calculators or spell-check. We use it wisely. We still review, fact-check, and make decisions."

**"What about data privacy?"**
→ "Important concern. Never input identifiable student data. Check your school's policy. Use AI for content creation, not sensitive information."

**"My school blocks everything. How can I use this?"**
→ "Talk to your IT department. Many schools are creating AI policies. Show them the benefits. Offer to pilot it."

**"What if it generates wrong information?"**
→ "Excellent critical thinking! AI can make mistakes. ALWAYS fact-check, especially in Science, Maths, History. You're the quality control."

## Reading the Room

### Signs of Engagement
✅ Focused on screens during activities
✅ Asking questions
✅ Excited chatter
✅ Volunteering to share
✅ Helping each other
✅ Staying on task

**Action:** Keep the pace, build on momentum

### Signs of Confusion
⚠️ Blank stares
⚠️ Not starting activities
⚠️ Asking basic questions repeatedly
⚠️ Clicking randomly

**Action:**
- Slow down
- Show again
- Provide step-by-step written instructions
- Pair confused participants with confident ones

### Signs of Boredom
⚠️ Checking phones
⚠️ Side conversations
⚠️ Rushing through without care
⚠️ Leaving early for breaks

**Action:**
- Increase pace
- Add challenge
- Ask them to help others
- Show more advanced features

## Your Facilitation Toolkit

### Phrases That Work

**To build confidence:**
- "You've got this!"
- "That's brilliant!"
- "Look how quickly you did that!"
- "You're a natural at this!"

**To manage time:**
- "You have [X] minutes left"
- "Start wrapping up..."
- "One more minute - finish your current task"

- "Great question - let's address that in..."
- "We'll come back to that"
- "Hold that thought for later"

**To handle struggle:**
- "That's normal - let's try again"
- "Technology can be frustrating"
- "Don't worry - you can't break it!"

**To celebrate:**
- "Look what you just created!"
- "Show the person next to you!"
- "This is amazing work!"

## Self-Care for Facilitators

### Energy Management
- Take deep breaths during breaks
- Drink water throughout
- Move around during activities (not just standing at front)
- Use participant work time for your mini-breaks

### Voice Care
- Don't shout over noise - use silence and proximity
- Vary your tone and pace
- Use the microphone if available
- Rest your voice during breaks

### Presence
- Be fully present - don't check your phone
- Make eye contact
- Learn names if possible
- Show genuine interest in their creations
